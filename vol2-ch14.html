A daily read of the Chinese tape

Chapter Thirty-Four · The Morning Given Back

by Dan Rivers · 2026-11-24
ONE
Narrative
The Day

At the opening on Tuesday morning the auction showed 4,962 names higher and 442 lower. ChiNext was indicated up 3.88 percent, the STAR composite up 3.94, Shenzhen 2.43, Shanghai 0.82. By the closing bell 3,498 names were lower and ChiNext had printed 2,278.99 — the exact price of its low for the day. Nothing in between had been hidden. The tape simply gave back, in six and a half hours, the whole of a gift it had been handed before the open.

The loudest number of the day came before the first trade. The honest one came at the last.

Of the six gauges in this fetch, three closed higher and three lower, but only one kept a large part of its opening gain. The Shanghai Composite finished at 3,548.37, up 0.27 percent, or 9.42 points, after opening at 3,567.84 and reaching 3,585.22. The Shenzhen Component closed at 10,423.60, down 0.29, having opened 2.43 percent higher. ChiNext ended at 2,278.99, down 0.05, after touching 2,376.34 — more than four percent above the prior close — and then sliding without a meaningful bid. The Beijing 50 closed at 793.60, down 0.22, also at its low of the day. The CSI 300 managed 3,963.82, up 0.14. The STAR 50 was the exception: 1,250.09, up 1.81 percent, the strongest broad gauge in the set.

Breadth tells the story more cleanly than any index. The opening count was 4,962 up against 442 down, with 175 unchanged. At midday it was 3,123 against 2,273. At the close it was 1,952 up and 3,498 down. Sixty-four names sealed at limit-up — the ten percent daily cap on most A-shares — against four at limit-down. A separate Shanghai-Shenzhen-only tally, which leaves out the Beijing exchange, put the same session at 1,838 up and 3,267 down; it is a different count and is not mixed here. The direction is not in doubt: the majority of prices finished lower, and the tape says so.

Turnover rose. The two markets traded ¥1.6551 trillion, about ¥102.2 billion more than the session before, with Shanghai contributing ¥771.9 billion and Shenzhen ¥883.2 billion. Volume rising on a day when most prices fell is not fear and not apathy; it is disagreement written large. For every buyer who took the opening gap there was a seller who used it, and the volume recorded both.

What was bought in the afternoon was not what had gapped in the morning. Consumer electronics gained 3.00 percent on the published industry classification, with an AI-phone and smart-speaker name up 20.01. Cultural and entertainment articles led the named industries at 3.55. Aerospace equipment added 2.99, naval equipment 2.64, insurance 2.21 and energy metals 2.00. Semiconductors, the closest thing to the overnight story, rose 1.53. Under the concept labels the bids went to humanoid robotics and physical AI, where two names reached a third consecutive limit-up and four more a second, and to an AI-comics theme that put two names at their second ceiling.

What was sold was Friday’s shelf. Precious metals fell 4.00 percent and finished with none of eleven names higher; industrial metals lost 3.44, minor metals 2.14, refining and trade 2.08, passenger cars 2.07, securities 1.51 and coal mining 1.18. On Friday precious metals had been the strongest named line in the market, up 5.25 percent. Two sessions later it was the weakest. The computing complex that had gapped on the news did not escape: an optical-module leader was up more than eleven percent intraday before its gain narrowed sharply, and a printed-circuit-board concept that opened strong slid through the afternoon.

As of 15:47 in Hong Kong, with the closing auction still to run, the Hang Seng stood at 22,212.12, down 0.77 percent, Hang Seng Tech at 3,465.97, down 1.20, and the HSCEI at 7,451.56, down 1.02. These are intraday readings, not closes. That market had opened higher — the Hang Seng up 1.09, Hang Seng Tech 2.05 — before reversing, and its intraday lows came close to fifty-two-week floors. The previous session’s close, on 23 November, was 22,383.74 and 3,508.00. Southbound flows via Stock Connect were reported only as intraday figures, roughly HK$4 billion near the open and HK$7.2 billion by midday; the full-session total was unavailable and none is quoted here.

Analysis
Why

The shape is the argument, and on Tuesday the shape was a gift refused. An index is a weighted sum; breadth is a headcount. At the opening auction the two agreed — nearly five thousand names higher and every major board gapped up. Then they parted. The headcount turned first, and the weighted sums followed through the afternoon on more volume than the day before. A gap filled in by the close is not a slow drift. It is a change of hands, recorded in size.

The cross-market fact belongs here as a coordinate, not a cause. The American session that finished before Shanghai opened was Monday 23 November, and it was extraordinary by any scale: the S&P 500 up 4.729 percent, the Nasdaq 6.507, the Dow 3.007, the Philadelphia semiconductor index 14.03. Nvidia rose 31.48 percent to $165.05, its largest one-day percentage gain since May 2003, adding $952 billion to its market value in a single session. Micron gained 19.04, TSMC 14.08. I put these beside the Shanghai open for scale. A price set in one country before dawn does not determine one set seven hours later.

The reason for that move is a dated item in the news file, not a trading signal. The chipmaker’s board voted to restore its founder to the offices of president, chief executive and director after a leave of some ten days. The A-share computing chain opened as if the vote had been a fresh order book, then spent the session proving it was not. Whole-market main-force money printed minus ¥3.781 billion, ending the previous session’s plus ¥26.97 billion. That is a mild outflow by the standards of this run — the prior six sessions included minus ¥52.08 billion and minus ¥31.11 billion — but its direction matters: the money that carried Monday did not carry Tuesday.

Inside the tape, the rotation is measurable. Components took the largest named main-force inflow at ¥3.109 billion, consumer electronics ¥2.486, communications equipment ¥1.403 and semiconductors ¥1.308. Yet communications equipment closed up just 0.07 percent after being more than six higher intraday. The two largest turnovers in the whole market, ¥28.583 billion and ¥24.975 billion, were the optical names whose prices faded. Volume stayed in the same names while the bid moved elsewhere. Securities, electric power and minor metals took the largest named outflows.

Several other items are dated and belong to the file. The exchanges lowered the minimum margin-financing ratio from 100 percent to 80 percent, effective 30 November, with one published estimate of nearly ¥400 billion of additional capacity; the central bank ran ¥516 billion of overnight reverse repos for a net injection of ¥16.5 billion; a wave of more than thirty share-buyback announcements followed Monday; and Washington tightened rules on remote access to advanced chips. None of these is used here to explain a price or to read a session that had not happened.

A gap is a price. What happens to it after the bell is a vote.

At the opening auction 4,962 names were higher. At the close 3,498 were lower, and ChiNext had finished at the exact price of its low.

Historical Parallels
The Record
The record shows where the road has been, not where it goes next.

Today’s fetch carries no verifiable historical analogue, and I will not manufacture one for a session that opened on a corporate announcement and gave the gap back in a single afternoon. What this letter’s own verified archive holds are two stretches with a measurable aftermath. Both are set down as history only.

One · 28 August 2023

On 28 August 2023 the stamp duty on share trading was halved. The Shanghai Composite opened 5.06 percent higher at 3,219.04; by the bell four-fifths of that gain was gone, and it closed at 3,098.64, barely 1.13 percent up. Turnover reached ¥1.1266 trillion, ¥360.3 billion above the session before, and more than 3,600 names finished higher against roughly 1,500 lower. What followed was slow arithmetic: the gap was filled, and by 23 October the index closed at 2,939.29 — 159 points, or 5.1 percent, below the announcement-day close. Tuesday’s opening shape is the same species — a large gap given back on rising volume. The difference is as large as the resemblance: 2023 opened on a domestic policy change and added ¥360.3 billion of fresh money, while Tuesday opened on a company announcement from another country, added ¥102.2 billion, and finished with more decliners than advancers. The archive records what happened in 2023 and says nothing about what follows Tuesday.

Difference worth keeping: 2023 had a policy gap, a positive closing headcount and ¥360.3 billion of new money; Tuesday had a corporate announcement, a negative closing headcount and ¥102.2 billion more turnover. No aftermath is claimed here.
In 2023 the gap was policy and the headcount stayed positive. In the optical boom the story outran the invoice. The archive keeps both outcomes and starts neither.
Two · 16 June to 31 July 2023

In June 2023 an optical-module leader closed at 170 yuan — more than fivefold from a year that opened near 27 — on a market value of ¥136.5 billion. Two days before the crest it had risen 14.99 percent in a single session. What followed was the invoice. On the night of 11 July a half-year preview showed net profit growth of 12 to 36 percent: good, but not the explosion a fivefold stock had been priced on, and the next session it fell more than six percent. By 31 July it closed 25 percent below its June peak on adjusted prices. Tuesday’s computing chain opened on a story of restored leadership rather than earnings, and its largest turnover went to names that could not hold the opening price. Difference worth keeping: the 2023 boom rested on a single customer chain, with no domestic pillar to catch it, and its unwind took weeks; Tuesday’s fade happened inside six hours, and the same names still drew the market’s largest turnover. The archive keeps both timelines and starts neither.

Difference worth keeping: 2023 measured a peak against a later earnings preview over weeks; Tuesday measured an opening auction against a closing bell in one session. The 25 percent figure belongs to July 2023 alone.

Coda
Last Word

At the opening the screens along a Shanghai street were filled with rising numbers. A noodle shop two doors down had not opened yet; the owner was still lifting the shutter. By four the numbers had turned and the shop had served three bowls. Neither screen nor shop kept a score.

The market had been given a morning and had handed it back. The closing bell is quieter than the opening one. It is also the only one that counts.
