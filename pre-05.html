A Daily Read of the Chinese Tape

Pre 5 · The Loudest Number

by Dan Rivers
Monday, October 5, 2026 · 中文题名 · 最响的数字
ONE Two and a Half Times Opening
8 Oct 2024:
¥3.46 trillion.
A session last month:
¥1.42 trillion.

On 8 October 2024, the first session after that year's Golden Week, ¥3.463 trillion changed hands across China's two exchanges. It was the largest single-day turnover the market had ever recorded. On an ordinary session at the end of September 2026, the same measure came to ¥1.422 trillion.

The loudest day in the market's history was roughly two and a half times a quiet one. That is the dynamic range a reader has to hold in mind, and it is why turnover — not the index — is usually the first thing a local trader looks at in the morning.

Turnover is the only figure on the screen that counts participation rather than opinion. An index level is a weighted argument about what things are worth; a quote is what someone asks. Turnover is money that actually moved. It cannot be posted, only paid. That makes it the most honest number on the board, and also the easiest one to misread.

TWO What the Number Counts Mechanism
Turnover counts
money that moved.
Prices only count
what people asked.

What locals call 成交额 is the sum of value traded across the session. The related measure, 换手率, divides that by the size of the float — how much of the thing changed hands, rather than how many yuan. Both are useful, and they disagree whenever the market itself grows: the same turnover spread over a larger market is a quieter day than it used to be. A record set in 2015 and a record set in 2024 are not the same size of event, even before inflation is discussed.

This is why the comparison is almost never against a fixed number. It is against the market's own recent selves — yesterday, the same hour of last week, the average of the last month. Terminals package that comparison as a ratio; the habit behind it is simple: loudness is relative to the room you are standing in.

One more caution of scope. The headline figure usually covers Shanghai and Shenzhen combined; a third exchange exists, is small, and is included or omitted depending on who is quoting. Differences of a few percent between two sources are usually scope, not error. Compare a number against the same source that produced it, and treat cross-year records with the suspicion they deserve.

There is a local saying, 量在价先 — volume precedes price. It is worth recording as vocabulary because you will hear it constantly, and worth recording as nothing more. It describes a sequence that sometimes happened. It is not a mechanism, and this chronicle does not use it to stand anywhere.

THREE Three Ways to Misread It Reading
The same price
on rising and
falling turnover
is not the same day.

The first misreading is to treat turnover as direction. It has none. A rising index on thickening trade and a rising index on thinning trade are different animals, and so are a falling index on heavy trade — money leaving in a hurry — and a falling index on thin trade, which is often just a room with nobody in it.

The second is to read the headline figure without asking what is sealed. On a day with hundreds of names pinned limit-up, the turnover in those names collapses: a stock locked at its ceiling barely trades, however much demand is queued behind it. The loudest board can contain the quietest stocks. The mechanism was described in Pre 02.

A fourth, and the subtlest: turnover can be concentrated. A loud total may be carried by a handful of names passing the same money back and forth while the other five thousand sit untouched. The number is honest about how much moved and silent about how widely. Locals watch which names sit at the top of the turnover table for exactly this reason — not because the list predicts anything, but because it tells you whose room you are actually standing in.

The third is to forget the calendar. Turnover thins before long holidays, year after year, because a position taken before a multi-day closure carries more nights than a position taken on an ordinary Tuesday. Pre 03 recorded this as one of the consequences of T+1: what cannot be closed tonight is not wanted tonight. A thin tape before National Day week is routine, not news.

FOUR Two Record Rooms Old accounts
28 May 2015:
¥2.17 trillion.
8 Oct 2024:
¥3.46 trillion.

Old account one. On 28 May 2015 the two exchanges traded ¥2.165 trillion — extraordinary for its day. Two weeks later the tape was quieter even as the index climbed: ¥2.085 trillion on 8 June, then ¥1.85 trillion on 15 June, the session after the index's high-water close of 5,166.35 on 12 June and the day it closed at 5,062.99, down 2.00 percent. One difference worth recording: that year's turnover was carried by borrowed money, which was later withdrawn by force. The same number of yuan does not mean the same thing under a different funding regime.

Old account two. 8 October 2024, the record: ¥3.463 trillion, on a session that opened more than ten percent higher and closed up 4.59 percent, followed the next day by a fall of 6.62 percent. That week was described in Pre 03. One difference worth recording: it was the fastest policy-driven re-rating in a decade; turnover of that width is the exception that proves the scale, not the scale itself.

What the two share is only this, and it is worth no more than it says: in both accounts, the loudest turnover of the era arrived near an extreme of feeling, not in the middle of one. That is a fact about those days. It is not a rule about the next one, and this chronicle will not use it as such.
