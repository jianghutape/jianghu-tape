A daily read of the Chinese tape

Chapter Thirty-Three · The Long Way Back

by Dan Rivers · 2026-11-23
ONE
Narrative
The Day

At 10:45 on Monday the Shanghai Composite was trading at 3,482, down 1.45 percent and below 3,500. By the close it stood at 3,538.95, up 0.15 percent. In between, five of the seven broad gauges in this fetch printed their lowest price in fifty-two weeks and then walked away from it. That was the mirror of Friday, when five boards made yearly lows and three closed on them. On Monday the lows came again, deeper, and this time the tape refused to keep them.

A low made at ten is a fact. What the market does with it by four is a different fact.

The Beijing 50 fell to 751.31 in the morning, a fifty-two-week low, and finished at 795.38, up 4.67 percent — the strongest of the seven. The Shenzhen Component touched 10,118.02 before ending at 10,454.37, up 1.93. ChiNext printed 2,188.45 and closed at 2,280.20, up 3.08. CSI 500 made 5,899.96 and finished at 6,128.99, up 2.05. Shanghai’s own low was 3,476.65. The CSI 300 fell to 3,901.49, just above its yearly floor of 3,894.47, and finished at 3,958.29, up 0.09. Only one of the seven gauges ended lower: the STAR 50, down 0.26 percent at 1,227.89, after having been down more than four percent during the session.

Under those closes, 4,658 names rose and 783 fell — roughly six in seven prices higher, the widest majority of this run. One hundred and fifty-one sealed at limit-up, the ten-percent daily cap on most A-shares, against seven at limit-down. At the opening auction the count had been 316 up and 5,020 down; at midday it was still 1,714 against 3,763. The turn began around eleven in the morning and did not stop. A separate Shanghai-Shenzhen-only tally, which leaves out the Beijing exchange, put the same session at 4,306 up and 780 down; it is a different count and is not mixed here.

On Friday one price in eighteen rose. On Monday six in seven did.

Turnover answers the only question a reversal leaves — was there money behind it? On Monday there was: ¥1.5529 trillion, about ¥130.7 billion more than Friday’s ¥1.4222 trillion. Shanghai contributed ¥738.5 billion and Shenzhen ¥814.38 billion. The same boards that had made yearly lows in the morning were being bought in size in the afternoon, and the volume had risen with them.

The bids went to the names that had been hit hardest. Communications equipment rose 5.67 percent on the published industry classification, with Tianfu Communications up 13.82 percent and one optical-module name drawing the largest single-stock main-force inflow in the market, ¥2.294 billion. Components gained 4.46 percent as printed-circuit-board names went to their ceilings. Automation equipment added 6.60 percent, with all 82 names in that bucket higher and a humanoid-robotics name up 20 percent on a market value above ¥260 billion. Digital media led the whole board at 9.18 percent, film and cinema 6.91, electric motors 6.78, precious metals 5.25 as spot gold climbed back above $3,600 in Asian trade.

What was sold was Friday’s bid. Insurance fell 2.86 percent, the weakest of the named industries; baijiu lost 1.98, state-owned big banks 1.20, coal mining 1.27 and passenger cars 1.53. On Friday those old seats were the only place a bid stood. On Monday they were the shelf money left. The whole-market main-force gauge printed plus ¥26.97 billion, ending seven consecutive sessions of net outflow; Friday’s had been minus ¥52.08 billion. Even the largest named outflow on Monday was under ¥0.5 billion, a measure of how little selling was concentrated anywhere.

As of 15:55 in Hong Kong, with the closing auction still to run, the Hang Seng stood at 22,381.54, down 0.13 percent, Hang Seng Tech at 3,511.78, up 0.01. These are intraday readings, not closes. The market there had made its own fifty-two-week lows earlier before recovering most of the way, a smaller version of the same V. Southbound flows via Stock Connect were reported only as a fifth straight session of net buying, with no figure attached, so no amount is quoted here.

TWO
Analysis
Why

Start with the shape, because the shape is the argument. An index is a sum with weights attached; breadth is a headcount. On Friday the two agreed on the way down and parted only at the largest weights. On Monday morning they agreed again — 316 names up against 5,020 down at the opening bell, five boards at yearly lows. Then they separated. The headcount turned first, around eleven, and the weighted sums followed through the afternoon. Volume rose with both. That is what a reversal looks like in the tape: not a quiet drift but a change of hands, recorded in more shares than the day before.

The cross-market fact belongs here, but as a coordinate and not a cause. The American session that finished before Shanghai opened was Friday 20 November — the last American session to finish before the Shanghai open on 23 November — and it was a rout in the very names that led Shanghai on Monday. The Philadelphia semiconductor index lost 10.73 percent and Nvidia fell 14.01 percent to $125.53; Micron and Broadcom lost more than twelve percent each, TSMC nearly nine and a half, the Nasdaq 5.283. In Shanghai the previous evening’s damage became the morning’s bid: communications equipment and components took the two largest named main-force inflows, ¥6.748 billion and ¥5.397 billion. I put these side by side. A price falling in New York on Friday does not set one in Shanghai at eleven on Monday; it gives the reader a scale.

Why the bid appeared has a dated background, not a mystery. Futures markets put the odds of a December rate increase in the United States above ninety percent, a sharp change from a week earlier, and that expectation was the reported backdrop to Friday’s selloff. It is a setting, not a lever. What changed in Shanghai was the location of the money: the whole-market main-force gauge swung from minus ¥52.08 billion on Friday to plus ¥26.97 billion on Monday, a single-day change of about ¥79 billion. Across the seven sessions before it that gauge had never once turned positive; Monday was the first positive print in eight.

Two government programmes gave the robotics names a reason that can be dated. One published a list of fifty-five agricultural uses for robots; another encouraged service robots and wearable devices in care for the old and the young. A public figure also offered a twelve-to-eighteen-month timetable for household tasks. These are dated items in the news file, not a reading of any session. The tape’s own evidence is simpler: 82 of 82 names in automation equipment rose, 26 of 26 in electric motors, and the strongest industry of the day was digital media. I note the catalyst and leave it there.

A reversal needs two things: a low someone will defend, and a headcount that turns.

Two things had not happened when this was written: the 23 November session across the Pacific had not begun, and the closing auction in the southern market had not produced a final print — which is why every reading above carries a clock time and none is called a close. A tape written at the close keeps that discipline. The margin balance in the news file is a 20 November figure, ¥2.1798 trillion, down ¥42.489 billion on the day; it belongs to Friday, not to Monday.

Five boards printed fifty-two-week lows before eleven in the morning; by the close six of the seven broad gauges were higher and 4,658 names had risen.

THREE
Historical Parallels
The Record
The record shows where the road has been, not where it goes next.

Today’s fetch carries no verifiable historical analogue, and I will not manufacture one for a session that made five yearly lows before eleven and erased them by the close. What this letter’s own verified archive holds are two stretches with a measurable aftermath. Both are set down as history only.

One · 16 March 2022

On 16 March 2022 the Shanghai Composite was down more than two percent in the morning and closed up 3.48 percent at 3,170.71, after a meeting of the financial committee was reported in the afternoon; 137 names went to limit-up on close to ¥1.2 trillion of turnover. The record keeps the aftermath: the V was not the bottom. Five weeks later, on 27 April, the Composite made the year’s low at 2,863.65 intraday and closed at 2,958.28. Monday’s shape — a long morning low, an afternoon bid, a broad headcount — resembles that day. The difference is as large as the resemblance: 2022 had a midday policy meeting behind the turn, and what followed it was five weeks lower. No meeting accompanied Monday’s turn, and this archive makes no statement about what follows it.

Difference worth keeping: 2022 had an announced meeting at the centre of the session and a lower low five weeks afterwards; Monday had no such meeting, seven limit-downs against 151 ceilings, and no claim here about its aftermath.
In 2022 the V came with a meeting and was not the bottom. In 2025 the chain fell first and rose two sessions later. The archive keeps both outcomes and starts neither.
Two · 27 January to 7 February 2025

On 27 January 2025 Nvidia fell more than 17 percent in one session on news of a low-cost Chinese AI model. When the A-share market reopened after the Spring Festival on 5 February, the optical-module chain went to its floors: Innolight fell 14.43 percent to 98.25, about half its 8 October 2024 high of 185.83. The record keeps the aftermath: by 7 February three module names had rebounded 5.0, 6.32 and 6.71 percent. Monday’s overnight backdrop was the same species — a steep fall in American semiconductors — and the same domestic chain took the largest inflows. The difference: 2025 had a two-week closure before the selling and a rebound over two sessions near a market high; Monday had no closure, the chain rose on the same day, and the market was making yearly lows.

Difference worth keeping: 2025 had a named shock, a two-week closure and a market near its highs; Monday’s turn was same-session, with five yearly lows made and erased in one tape. A February rebound is a fact about February 2025.

The record stands for comparison.

FOUR
Coda
Last Word

At four o’clock a securities branch on a Shanghai street pulls down its shutter. Two doors along, a stall weighs the last vegetables of the day. Neither has a view on a fifty-two-week low. Somewhere in the offices above them a large order changed hands on Monday, and the tape kept the volume and not the name.

At ten that morning the Composite had been below 3,500. At four it was above 3,538 and the stall lights were on. The market had walked itself back. That was all the day promised.
