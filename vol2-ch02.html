A daily read of the Chinese tape

Chapter Twenty-Two · Distance

by Dan Rivers · 2026-11-06
ONE
Narrative
The Day

The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 12.25 percent on the night of 5 November — its heaviest single session since 16 March 2020. All thirty of its components closed lower. Micron lost 17.46 percent; Nvidia lost 9.26. The volatility index rose 69.48 percent to 28.15. And in the same city, on the same night, the index of Chinese companies listed in New York closed up 0.43 percent, NetEase four percent higher, JD.com three.

Two verdicts, hours apart, on one event. Shanghai’s task on Friday morning was to decide which it believed.

The opening auction answered first. The Composite priced at 3,612.15, a gap of 2.11 percent. It was very nearly the low: 3,610.20 printed within the first minutes. Then it climbed, through the morning and past the lunch break, to 3,660.49 in the afternoon. It closed at 3,649.18 — down 1.10 percent, and thirty-seven points above where it had begun.

ChiNext took the same road and gave back more of it. The growth board opened at 2,438.72 — its low, set on the first tick. It ran to 2,506.97, then returned forty-two points of that recovery. It closed at 2,465.23, down 3.96 percent. Shanghai kept three-quarters of its rebound. Shenzhen kept two-fifths.

One instruction, two exchanges. Shanghai read it as a rotation. Shenzhen read it as a repricing.

Breadth is what made the day legible. Seven hundred and fifty-eight stocks closed higher and four thousand seven hundred and forty-one closed lower. Thirty-three finished sealed at their ceilings, fifteen at their floors — against eighty-six ceilings and none at all on Thursday.

The selling was concentrated. Components fell 7.13 percent, with Aohong Electronics and Dongshan Precision sealed at their floors and the two largest circuit-board names, WUS Printed Circuit and Shengyi Technology, sharply lower. Communications equipment lost 5.75. Semiconductors lost 3.35. Medical services lost 4.18, and electronics chemicals, robotics and gaming all finished among the worst of the board.

The buying was somewhere else entirely, and it was old. Power generation rose 1.30 percent with three small utilities sealed at their ceilings. Banks rose 1.05, Shanghai Rural Commercial up more than four. Planting and forestry rose on a day the index was down more than two percent at its worst — Qiule Seed up 13.49, Wanxiang Denong at its second ceiling in three sessions. Railway and highway names, utilities, corn, modified seed: a list that reads like a portfolio assembled in 2011 and never reopened.

Turnover printed 1.6578 trillion yuan, 460 billion below Thursday’s 2.118. Two sessions above two trillion, then this. The money did not run; it withdrew — and the board recorded it in the one column that cannot be argued with.

TWO
Analysis
Why

The two verdicts were not a contradiction. They were a sorting, and the sorting had one rule: distance.

The event was specific. American networks projected a Congress inclined to slow the permitting of data centres, and UBS put a figure on the consequence — roughly thirty percent of the capacity the United States had been expected to add by 2028 could be exposed to a pause in construction. Every asset whose revenue is booked against that calendar was repriced on the spot: optical modules, copper-clad laminate, circuit boards, memory. Every asset whose revenue is booked against something else was not.

Which is why the Golden Dragon index rose while the semiconductor index lost an eighth of its value in a night. A platform company’s revenue arrives as advertising, commissions and game sales. A Chinese optical module maker’s revenue arrives as a purchase order from a hyperscaler in Virginia that has not yet been permitted. Same country of listing; different landlord.

The rule held inside the semiconductor sector too, which is why “semiconductors fell 3.35 percent” is a sentence that conceals more than it says. The largest single net purchase in the entire market on Friday was Northern Huachuang, a domestic fab-equipment maker, at 610 million yuan. Hua Hong, the foundry, rose as much as sixteen percent intraday after a third-quarter report that lifted net profit 138 percent. Both sit inside the sector that fell. Neither sells into an American building permit: one sells machines to fabs whose construction schedule is set in Beijing, the other sells wafers to anyone holding a Chinese chip design.

The exits were large and few. Components gave up 5.43 billion yuan of main-force money. Semiconductors and communications equipment each lost more than four and a half billion. Medical services lost 1.83. The entrances were small: power took in 942 million, banks 853. In total, the defensive side of the board received about a fifth of what the hardware side handed over. That asymmetry is the honest description of Friday. It was not a rotation of equal weight. It was a withdrawal with a partial reinvestment, and it shows in the index arithmetic: Shanghai down one percent while Shenzhen fell nearly four.

The shape of the selling mattered more than its size. Fifteen stocks finished at their floors out of 4,741 that fell. In April 2025, an overnight shock of comparable violence produced 2,902 floors in a single session. A repricing and a panic are distinguished by exactly this count: in a repricing the bid is thin, but it is quoted all day.

The policy plumbing was visible. A report on financial work delivered to the legislature said Central Huijin would be supported in playing the role of a quasi-stabilisation fund, and that channels for medium- and long-term money to enter the market would be widened. At midday, seven of the companies at the centre of the fall — three optical names, a components maker, a storage maker, a connector maker and one other — published announcements inside the same hour, all citing the buyback-and-re-lending facility. By three o’clock they had been read and set aside; the optical leaders closed sharply lower regardless. After the bell the swap facility was drawn again, with China International Capital reporting purchases under its third allocation and Galaxy Securities completing a first buyback financing and its first purchases.

None of that is a price. It is the plumbing beneath the price, and on Friday it ran while the hardware complex was marked down.

Hong Kong, on the same clock, printed the same trade at two-thirds the amplitude. At 15:38 there, with the closing auction still to run, the Hang Seng was 1.49 percent lower at 24,427.62. Hang Seng Tech was 2.36 percent lower at 4,056.30. It had opened 1.58 percent down, narrowed to about one percent by the lunch break, and widened again through the afternoon. The names at the bottom were the names at the bottom here: the optical names down fourteen and twelve, the circuit-board pair down eleven and ten. Southbound money, which had net-sold HK$21.3 billion on Thursday, was a net buyer of HK$2.17 billion by noon.

A shock is not a verdict. It is a measurement of how far each asset sits from the thing that changed.

Distance accounts for the whole board, from the Golden Dragon’s green to the three utilities sealed at their ceilings. What it does not account for is what comes next. For that, this file keeps older receipts.

THREE
Historical Parallels
The Record
The record shows where the road has been, not where it goes next.
One · 17 March 2020

The night before, 16 March 2020, the Philadelphia semiconductor index closed 15.9 percent lower — still its heaviest single session on record. All thirty of its components fell; six lost more than a fifth. The Nasdaq fell 12.32 percent, and the Dow and the S&P 500 fell further. It was the third circuit breaker of that month.

Shanghai opened that morning at 2,796.28, fell to 2,715.22 inside the first hour, turned at ten o’clock, and had all three indices green before the lunch break. The afternoon surrendered part of it. The Composite closed at 2,779.64, down 0.34 percent. ChiNext closed higher, up 0.36. Turnover was 818 billion yuan, and northbound investors sold a net six billion for a fourth consecutive session.

What followed: on 18 March the Composite gave up 1.83 percent to 2,728.76. On 19 March it printed 2,646.80 — the low of that spring, and 133 points beneath the close of the V-shaped day. It finished the month at 2,750.30, still under the level that resilience had defended.

Difference worth keeping: in 2020 the shock landed at the end of a three-week liquidation, and Shanghai had already fallen 3.40 percent in the session before the largest American drop, so the V-day was a bounce inside a decline that was nearly spent. Friday’s gap arrived into a market that had just spent two sessions recovering — a different position from which to be surprised. The record also notes that in 2020 the low came two sessions after the display of courage, not on the day of it.
In 2020 the buy-the-dip morning closed 0.34 percent lower and the real low was 133 points below it, two sessions later.
Two · 14 September 2022

The night of 13 September 2022, American inflation printed 8.3 percent against 8.1 expected. The Nasdaq fell 5.16 percent, its worst session in more than two years. Shanghai opened at 3,224.68 and dipped to 3,221.96 inside the hour; it recovered to 3,250.80, then closed at 3,237.54, down 0.80. ChiNext lost 1.84 to 2,503.82, most of it from a single battery maker down almost five percent.

Turnover that day was 722.8 billion yuan, the second-lowest of the year, above only 9 May. Breadth read 1,147 up against 3,621 down — and, in the count that matters here, forty-one ceilings against ten floors. Northbound sold a net 1.41 billion yuan.

What followed: the index did not stop. It closed on 31 October at 2,893.48 — 344 points, or 10.6 percent, beneath the close of the resilient session — the first month-end of that year below 3,000. The turn, in November, came from a change in policy rather than from the count of floors.

Difference worth keeping: 2022’s shock was a single macro number, which reprices every asset at once. Friday’s was narrower and, for one industry, deeper — a revenue assumption revised by an election. That narrowness is why thirty-three stocks still finished at their ceilings while the index that owns the industry fell nearly four percent. What the two accounts share is only this: a session that absorbs a shock well is not the same event as a low.

In both accounts the session that absorbed the shock was not the low.

FOUR
Coda
Last Word

At a quarter to three on Friday afternoon, Luyang Energy-Saving was sealed at its floor. Fifteen minutes later it was sealed at its ceiling — the second straight session in which it had crossed the entire board, its fifth limit-up in six days, some 43 percent added in five.

In a market where four thousand seven hundred names fell, one name used both edges of the board inside a quarter of an hour. That is not a signal, nor a forecast. It is the only thing this file can state flatly about a day like Friday: whatever else was for sale, there was still a price at which somebody wanted to own something.
