A daily read of the Chinese tape

Chapter Nine · The Many

by Dan Rivers · 2026-10-20
ONE
Narrative
The Day

At 2:56 on Tuesday afternoon, with four minutes left, the Shanghai 50 exchange-traded fund was still working. By the closing bell 7.53 billion yuan of it had changed hands — the heaviest day for that fund since 29 January. The fifty companies inside it had spent the session being sold: insurers, banks, the big liquor names, the part of this market that pays a dividend and asks nothing of anyone.

Somebody bought the whole index on a day when the market sold its largest members. That was the morning's puzzle and the afternoon's too.

It was also the exact reverse of the day before. On Monday the Shanghai Composite rose 14.64 points and 3,129 stocks fell. On Tuesday it rose 4.45 points and 3,778 stocks rose. The same headline, twice, describing two different countries.

The index opened at 3,718.44, below Monday's close, sank to 3,702.44 before lunch, then climbed all afternoon to 3,740.07 and settled at 3,735.37. Four and a half points, a twelfth of one percent. The Shenzhen Component lost 0.14 percent and the ChiNext 0.26, while the STAR 50, the board for the hardest technology, gained 1.00 and the Beijing 50, where the smallest companies list, gained 3.88 — the widest move on any board in the country. Turnover was 1.4561 trillion yuan, 18.6 billion more than Monday: barely a rounding error, and the smallest increase in a week.

  • STAR 50 1,342.42, +1.00% · Beijing 50 982.87, +3.88% · CSI 300 4,152.11, −0.54%
  • Advancing 3,778 · declining 1,668 · unchanged 129 · suspended 7

  • Combined turnover ¥1.4561 trillion, against ¥1.4375 trillion on Monday

What rose was the machine. The human-robot complex — speed reducers, joint actuators, machine vision, the whole parts list of a body nobody has yet been sold at retail — put roughly fifty stocks at their ceiling or above ten percent, the second session of a run that began on Monday. Changying Precision, a connector and housing manufacturer that spent two years being a phone-parts company, closed 19.99 percent higher at 24.67. Jiachen Intelligent, listed where the wider ceiling applies, took all thirty. Fanluo Digital, which makes software for rendering factories, took twenty. Alongside them, the smaller end of edge computing went up together: Hengxuan Technology and Beken both at their limits, the module makers behind them bid up on the idea that artificial intelligence is moving out of the data centre and into devices people carry.

What fell was the establishment. Insurance and banks led the decline — precisely the two sectors that had been Monday's refuge. Coal fell, Dayou Energy down more than eight percent. Autos fell, Jinlong Motor nearly five. Gold miners, shipping, aviation and defence all gave ground, and the white goods that had risen for a fortnight handed part of it back. The CSI 300, which is the index most readers outside this market would recognise, lost 0.54 percent.

The tape has one honest way to describe Tuesday: the index is an average of the few, and the few had a bad day. Everything else in the country had a good one.

Monday: index up, market down. Tuesday: index flat, market up. The two lines have not agreed all month. They simply changed places.
TWO
Analysis
Why

The reason arrived from Texas, in a filing, at the weekend.

On Monday in New York, Tesla submitted the materials for its 2026 annual shareholder meeting. In the annual report attached to them, the company wrote — in a document rather than at a demonstration, for the first time — that it had readied its third-generation humanoid robot for mass production, that it was building the first production line, and that it was expanding the data-centre capacity used to train the machine. The same document did not give a volume, a price, or a date for delivery.

That is the entire content of the news, and it moved a supply chain on the other side of the world by double digits. This is the part of the market that cannot buy Tesla and buys instead the companies that could sell it a screw. The parts named in the coverage are unglamorous and specific: planetary roller screws, harmonic reducers, linear actuators. Two of the companies telephoned by the local press declined to confirm any link to the programme. The shares went up anyway, which tells you what the buyers were actually purchasing: not a contract, but a place in the queue.

What made the queue worth joining was emptiness. The robot index had fallen about 15 percent from its 18 September high to 15 October, and had bounced on Monday before Tuesday's continuation. A theme that has been sold for a month needs very little news to be re-owned. The filing was not the cause; the filing was the permission.

Money moved accordingly. Electronics took in more than 14 billion yuan of main-force net inflow — the vendor measure of the difference between large buy orders and large sell orders, which counts size and not motive — with machinery and computer software each above 5 billion, pharmaceuticals above 4, and telecom above 3. Out went the financials: non-bank institutions and banks each surrendered more than 3 billion, with utilities and defence above 1. Consumer electronics and the human-robot complex each drew more than 7 billion. In one detail worth keeping: household appliances recorded an eleventh consecutive day of net inflow while appliance stocks fell. Size of order and direction of price are different questions, and Tuesday asked both at once.

The leverage, meanwhile, is still leaving. Margin financing — money borrowed against stock to buy more stock — stood at 2.4455 trillion yuan at Monday's settlement, down 0.26 percent and lower for a seventh straight session, with the short side at 29.17 billion and the combined balance below its twenty-day average. The credit that carried the autumn's technology trade higher has been shrinking every day for two weeks. Tuesday's rally was paid for in cash.

Then there is the wrapper. In the final hour three broad index funds traded extraordinary volume: the Shanghai 50 fund's 7.53 billion yuan, the heaviest since January; a CSI 300 fund at 4.22 billion, more than ten times the day before; a second CSI 300 fund at 2.6 billion, more than double. Buying a basket is how a large institution expresses a view on a market without expressing one on any company inside it, and it is also how a very large order avoids being read line by line. What the tape records is narrower and stranger than any interpretation of it: on a day when the largest companies were sold, the index that holds them changed hands in size.

Overnight, the backdrop had finally turned helpful. The American session that closed before Shanghai opened on Tuesday — Monday 19 October in New York — had the Nasdaq up 1.11 percent and the S&P 500 up 0.41, with the Dow fractionally lower. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 1.61 percent, ending a run of eight consecutive declines that had taken roughly two trillion dollars of chip value with it. The dollar index was 101.77 late on Tuesday afternoon in Asia, 0.21 percent softer, and copper was 110,820 yuan a tonne. The chip index's eight-day fall stopped the night before Shanghai opened, and the first thing Shanghai did with the news was buy the parts of itself that had been sold hardest.

The largest order of the day bought a basket. A basket has no opinion about any company inside it.
THREE
Historical Parallels
The Record

On Monday the few rose and the many fell. On Tuesday the many rose and the few fell. The index, being an average of the few, barely noticed either.

Two entries in the record have Tuesday's shape: the heavyweights sold, the broad list bought, the headline unmoved. One of them is exactly one year old this week.

One · 20 to 31 October 2025

A year ago this same week the market did a larger version of Tuesday. The Composite closed at 3,863.89 on Monday 20 October, up 0.63 percent, and then ran for eight sessions: 3,950.31 by the Friday, 4,016.33 the following Wednesday, and an intraday 4,025.70 on the Thursday. It closed that week at 3,954.79, down 0.81 percent on the last day. The growth boards ran further and gave back more. The ChiNext went from 2,993.45 to an intraday 3,331.86 on 30 October and closed the month at 3,187.53; the STAR 50 went from 1,367.90 to 1,496.06 on 28 October and closed at 1,415.53. Over the full fortnight the Composite gained 3.00 percent, the ChiNext 8.59, the STAR 50 3.84 — and every one of the three was lower on the final day than on the day before the peak.

Difference to note: a year ago the door was opened by a policy document — the autumn plenum's language on science and self-reliance — and the index began that week 128 points above where it finished this one. Tuesday's door was opened by a company filing that contained no numbers at all. The shape rhymed; the substance did not.
Two · 18 February to 9 March 2021

On 18 February 2021 the crowded trade in this market reached its high-water mark. The CSI 300 touched 5,930.91 that day and the ChiNext 3,476.00; the Composite's own peak, 3,731.69, came in the same session. What followed was not a single break but a rotation, executed at speed. By 9 March the CSI 300 had closed at 4,917.91, down 17.1 percent from that high; the ChiNext at 2,608.24, down 25.0; the Composite at 3,328.31, down 10.8. In the same thirteen sessions a great many smaller companies rose, because the money leaving the biggest names had to land somewhere.

Difference to note: in February 2021 the heavyweights stood at a valuation peak and margin debt was at a record, so the selling that followed was a crowd going out through one door. On Tuesday the benchmark sat roughly 30 percent below that 2021 high and the margin balance had fallen seven sessions running. Crowded and expensive are different risks, and the comparison here is about who owned the index, not what it cost.
The record shows where the road has been, not where it goes next.

Both entries describe one mechanism from opposite ends: the index and the market are two objects, and a session can belong to one and not the other. Neither entry says which object Tuesday belonged to. The record stands for comparison; it does not answer that.

Both entries are about ownership, not price. Who holds the index is a fact. What the index is worth is an argument.
FOUR
Coda
Last Word

Hong Kong, another exchange inside this same country, answered differently: the Hang Seng fell 1.32 percent to 23,867.74 in its first session since Friday, Monday having been a holiday there, with the Hang Seng Tech Index down 1.52. Shanghai's small caps and Hong Kong's largest companies spent the same six hours moving opposite ways. They are different lists.

Among Tuesday's fifty-seven stocks at their ceiling were at least two that make food — a seasoning house from Fujian and a dairy from Inner Mongolia. Neither has anything to do with a robot. Both were bought by people who had watched the machine trade run for two days and wanted the nearest thing still moving.

In Beijing the plane trees turned all at once this week, an avenue going yellow in four days, on a schedule nobody published. By Friday it is the only thing left to look at.
