A daily read of the Chinese tape

Chapter Twenty · After One

by Dan Rivers · 2026-11-04
ONE
Narrative
The Day

ChiNext printed its low for the day at 2,376.28 on Wednesday morning. The floor it broke had been set at three o’clock the previous afternoon, at 2,376.39 — eleven hundredths of a point lower, less than five thousandths of one percent, which no chart of the year will ever show. The exchange recorded it as a fifty-two-week low all the same, and then the number was abandoned.

By noon the session looked like a third day of the same story. At 12:01 the Shanghai Composite was 0.10 percent lower. More than three thousand seven hundred stocks were down. The STAR 50, the index that owns the wafers and the memory and the optical modules, had spent the morning being sold.

Then, a little after one o’clock, the tape turned. Not a drift — a one-way street. The Composite climbed from a tenth of a percent in the red to 2.16 percent in the green and stopped: 3,686.17, which was both the highest price of the day and the last one. ChiNext, having made a yearly low before lunch, closed 6.40 percent higher at 2,544.73. The STAR 50 added 9.92 percent. The Shenzhen Component rose 4.22, the Beijing exchange 4.56, and the SSE 50 — the fifty largest companies, the ones with the banks in them — rose 1.45 and was the weakest of the lot.

Four thousand six hundred and sixty-seven stocks closed higher and seven hundred and seventy-nine closed lower, with a hundred and thirty-two unchanged. A hundred and twenty-one finished sealed at their ceilings; ten at their floors. At lunchtime the count had been the other way round.

A hundred and twenty-one ceilings against ten floors. The asymmetry is not sentiment. It is a shortage of sellers at any price below the ceiling.

The buying went to one chain, end to end. Semiconductors rose 11.41 percent and a hundred and eighty-one of their hundred and eighty-three names finished green: Jiangfeng, Tuojing, Kingsemi, Qiangyi and Jingyi sealed at twenty percent, NAURA and Tongfu at ten, and the semiconductor funds themselves closed at their ceilings. Electronic chemicals rose 10.55 with thirty-five of thirty-six up. Components rose 9.14 with sixty of sixty-one. Communications equipment rose 8.91 with eighty-five of eighty-six, Eoptolink up 13.35 and the optical maker Zhongji Innolight up more than ten — the two names that had been sold hardest twenty-four hours earlier.

Precious metals rose 10.06 percent with all eleven names higher, after spot gold added two percent into the afternoon and printed 4,009 dollars an ounce at 13:57. Medical services rose 7.83, with WuXi AppTec and Asymchem sealed at their ceilings.

What fell was October. Liquor lost 2.05 percent and two of its nineteen names finished green. The big state banks lost 1.85 with none of six up. Coal, insurers, dairy, condiments: red, on a day when four thousand six hundred stocks rose. The heaviest weights in the Composite were sold all afternoon and the Composite still closed at its high, which is what broad buying looks like when the weights are not doing the work.

TWO
Analysis
Why

Two point zero three seven seven trillion yuan changed hands. That is three hundred and thirty-four billion more than Tuesday, and the first close above two trillion since 22 September — twenty-five sessions in which the tape had been shrinking, session by session, toward one point four. Volume is the only number on the board that a single buyer cannot fake.

The flows inside it were lopsided in a way that is easy to misread. Semiconductors took in 19.40 billion yuan of main-force money, the largest single-sector intake in weeks; communications equipment took 7.46 billion and components 4.88, so three links of one chain absorbed more than 31 billion between them. The largest exit of the day was liquor, at 1.43 billion — a thirteenth the size of the largest entrance. Money did not leave this market on Wednesday. It changed sides.

The biggest exit was a thirteenth the size of the biggest entrance. That is not distribution. That is a queue forming at one door.

Set the two sessions side by side and the shape is almost algebraic. On Tuesday the hardware chain was sold and the shelters were bought: communications equipment down 8.37 percent with twelve of eighty-six names green, while liquor, insurers and the big banks rose. On Wednesday every one of those signs reversed. The tape has now printed both sides of a single trade on consecutive days, which is less a rotation than a market arguing with itself in public.

The overnight tape did not settle it. New York closed mixed on Tuesday: the S&P 500 up 0.36 percent and the Nasdaq up 0.83, the Philadelphia semiconductor index up 2.63, Micron up 2.58, Nvidia up 2.29 — and the Dow down 0.38 with Apple down 1.90. And the same morning that Shanghai was making its yearly low in ChiNext, Seoul was selling the identical product: SK Hynix down six percent, Samsung Electronics down four, Kioxia down eleven in Tokyo, SoftBank down seven. If New York had been the cause, the Korean morning would not have looked like that.

The domestic policy file offered a different sort of evidence. The central bank said it will raise the reserve requirement on banks’ deposits held in currencies other than the yuan from 4 percent to 6 percent on 15 November — a tool it reaches for when the currency is being pushed in one direction — and the offshore yuan slipped to 6.676 on the news. The ten-year government bond yield went below 1.6 percent, to 1.5975. Huatai Securities disclosed that it had taken part in the third tender under the swap facility that lets brokers post bonds for cash. Cheap funding, a currency being managed, and a bond market pricing something other than growth: that is the background, and it did not change at one o’clock.

Hong Kong spent the morning disagreeing and the afternoon agreeing. At 12:01 its tech index was down 1.02 percent. At 15:37 there, with the closing auction still to run, the Hang Seng was 23,875.63, up 1.49, and Hang Seng Tech was up 1.71, with SMIC up seven percent and Hua Hong up close to seven. Southbound money had bought HK$3 billion by 14:44, a second consecutive day of net buying after Tuesday’s HK$23.4 billion ended fourteen sessions of selling. Same clock, same semiconductor complex, and this time the same direction.

What deserves attention is not the size of the move but where it stopped. There was no fade. On a two-trillion-yuan day, with the index at its high, the last print was the high print — which says something about who was still bidding at ten to three, and nothing at all about the open the next morning.

THREE
Historical Parallels
The Record
The record shows where the road has been, not where it goes next.
One · 16 March 2022

A Wednesday that opened higher, fell all morning, and reversed in the afternoon. The Composite had lost 4.95 percent the day before. On this day it opened at 3,107.67 and slid to 3,023.30 before the afternoon. It then climbed for the rest of the session to close at 3,170.71, up 3.48 percent, with the Shenzhen Component gaining 4.02 and ChiNext 5.20. More than four thousand stocks rose against fewer than four hundred that fell, and about a hundred and forty sealed at their ceilings. Turnover came in at 1.19 trillion yuan. The Hang Seng rose 9.08 percent the same day, its largest single session since October 2008.

What followed: the next session added 1.40 percent to 3,215.04. Then the reversal gave way to six weeks of drift and disappointment. By 26 April the Composite closed at 2,886.43, its lowest since May 2020 — two hundred and eighty-four points beneath the close of the day the buying began. The following morning it traded at 2,863.65 before recovering.

Difference worth keeping: 2022 had a sentence. The Financial Stability Committee met over the lunch hour, and the tape repriced within thirty minutes of the text being read. Nothing in Wednesday’s published record is stamped at the hour the buying started. A rally with a name attached can be audited; one without a name is only a price.
The 2022 reversal was correct for one session and wrong for six weeks. Both facts are in the record.
Two · 6 February 2024

A Tuesday that began in liquidation. After six consecutive down days the Composite opened at 2,680.48, fell to 2,669.67 in the morning, and turned when the state fund announced before the open that it had widened its purchases of index funds, with the securities regulator adding three measures at midday. It closed at 2,789.49, up 3.23 percent. The Shenzhen Component and ChiNext each rose more than six percent; the Beijing exchange rose 9.50. Turnover was 926.3 billion yuan. Three thousand eight hundred and twenty-nine stocks rose. About a hundred and forty-five sealed at their ceilings — while roughly two hundred fell more than nine percent on the same day.

What followed: the next session added 1.44 percent to 2,829.70 and closed on its high. Eight consecutive up days then carried the Composite back above 3,000 on 23 February, at 3,004.88.

Difference worth keeping: in 2024 the index rose while two hundred small companies were being sold at any price — the index and the count of stocks disagreed. On Wednesday they agreed, and the disagreement sits somewhere else, between what was bought this week and what was bought all through October. The other difference is a name: 2024 had a buyer prepared to be quoted. Wednesday had none.

The morning and the afternoon were both honest. They were simply priced by different people.

FOUR
Coda
Last Word

Eleven hundredths of a point. On a chart it is nothing — the line touches a level and turns, and no reader will ever know that a floor set at three o’clock one afternoon was broken at half past nine the next morning by one hundredth of a percent and then handed back.

But somebody’s order filled there. That is the whole of it: the market keeps two kinds of records, the ones that matter and the ones that print, and on Wednesday morning they were eleven hundredths of a point apart.

By three o’clock the same index was up six and a half percent. It is the same tape. It is not the same day.
