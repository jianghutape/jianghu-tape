A daily read of the Chinese tape

Chapter Seventeen · Eighty-Six

by Dan Rivers · 2026-10-30
ONE
Narrative
The Day

In the language of the American kitchen, to eighty-six something is to strike it from the menu: the scallops are gone, stop sending the order. On Friday the exchange eighty-sixed eighty-six stocks — closed them at their floors and left them there. Forty-nine others finished sealed at their ceilings. On a day when the headline index fell only 1.44 percent, nearly twice as many stocks were locked at the bottom as at the top.

The morning had a shape of its own. Shanghai opened 1.25 percent lower and within minutes slipped below the 3,600 line, printing a low of 3,595.26. Then it climbed — all morning, with the breadth of the market improving hour by hour, and by the midday break the Composite was down 0.30 percent. Nearly a full point of the gap had been repaired. The afternoon handed almost all of it back: the close was 3,599.02, down 1.44 percent, less than four points above where the first hour had bottomed.

What the sectors did all day is a larger fact than what the index did in the afternoon. The hardware chain came apart in the order these things come apart: communications equipment 7.36 percent lower, components 6.21, semiconductors 5.32, consumer electronics 4.26. In the middle of that, Eoptolink — the optical-module maker whose price has become the chain’s thermometer — turned over ¥21.6 billion, more than any other stock on either exchange, and fell more than ten percent. Victory Giant, one of the board makers underneath it, fell by about the same.

A morning low of 3,595.26 and a close of 3,599.02. Less than four points apart, with a whole session of recovery in between, and every one of those points given back.
The most heavily traded stock in the country fell more than ten percent. The crowd left through one door and paid the door.

The other side of the board was stocked with things nobody has called a growth story in a decade. Four liquor names finished at their ceilings — Luzhou Laojiao, Gujing Gongjiu, Yingjia Gongjiu, Huangtai — and the sector closed 2.13 percent higher. Banks were green, coal miners were green, and so were the shipping lines, the hotel kitchens and the hospital groups, several of them by more than the liquor sector managed. Thursday’s best sector, aerospace and defence equipment, gave back 3.90 percent on Friday: a one-day trip, and the day had ended.

Five to one is not a rotation. A rotation lifts one column while it lowers another, and the column it lowers is where the money came from. Five to one means the column being lifted is small enough to fit inside the one being lowered: 893 names up, 4,602 down, and most of the board simply repriced downward while two dozen quiet corners of it were bid.

TWO
Analysis
Why

The New York session that ended on Friday morning in Shanghai was not an alarm. The Dow lost 0.25 percent and the S&P 0.30. The Nasdaq’s 1.38 percent fall was concentrated where it has been concentrated all month, in the chip complex, with the Philadelphia semiconductor index off 1.42 and Micron down a little under five percent. Apple rose. Gold gave up less than half a percent.

That is a memory-pricing signal, and it explains why the semiconductor chain opened weak. It does not explain eighty-six names sealed at their floors. A memory signal reaches foundries and module makers and the people who finance them; it does not reach cinema chains and mobile-game studios and the machine-vision companies selling to factory floors. Something else was being sold on Friday, and it was sold by people who had bought it for reasons that no longer applied.

Look at what finished at its floor. Five theatre and film names, including Wento and Hengdian. A game publisher, 37 Interactive. Around them, the names that did not quite reach their floors: short-form video and machine vision companies down between twelve and fourteen percent, an online brokerage-software name down more than ten. None of them has a memory contract, a wafer allocation or an order book in the AI build-out. What they share is a different thing: each was the crowded end of a trade that had been crowded for a month, held by people who owned it because other people owned it.

Then consider the shield, and what it was worth. Banks closed higher. Coal closed higher. Liquor closed higher by more than two percent, with four names at their ceilings. In an older market that combination produced a flat index. On Friday it produced minus 1.44, which tells you where the index’s centre of gravity has moved: the technology complex is now heavy enough to overrule a green banking sector.

A green bank sector and a red index on the same afternoon. Whatever the index says it is made of, the tape says something else.

The flow numbers say the same thing in a duller voice. Semiconductors gave up ¥5.63 billion of main-force money and communications equipment ¥5.27 billion; consumer electronics and components lost smaller amounts. The precious-metals complex fell more than four percent, on a night when the metal itself gave up less than half of one. Into the other side went liquor, medical services and batteries. CATL drew ¥1.09 billion of net buying, the largest single inflow on the board, in a session when its own sector was not the story.

Here is the detail worth keeping. The two blocks that rose most reliably, banks and coal, both recorded small net outflows of main-force money — a hundred million here, a hundred million there, small enough to be noise, and identical in direction. Green and being sold into. A defence that is bought by people who intend to stay looks different from a defence used as a waiting room, and this one looked like a waiting room.

The exchange in Hong Kong did not agree, and its disagreement is the most instructive thing on the tape. At 15:37 there, with the closing auction still to run, the Hang Seng stood at 23,306.44, up 1.51 percent. Hang Seng Tech was up 0.34 — a hundred and seventeen basis points behind its parent, and that gap is the whole story.

The Hang Seng’s heavyweights are banks, oil, shipping and telecom: the same things Shanghai was buying. Its technology index is built from consumer platforms, and those rose too, just barely. So both exchanges reached the same verdict on Friday — sell the hardware, hold the coupon — and printed opposite signs, because one benchmark is constructed from the part of this economy that pays a dividend and the other from the part that promises a future. The index you happen to quote decides whether the day was red or green.

It was the last session of October. Turnover rose to ¥1.674 trillion, about ¥117.9 billion more than Thursday, on a day when four thousand six hundred names fell. Volume up, price down, and the door narrower at the end of the queue than at the start.

THREE
Historical Parallels
The Record
The record shows where the road has been, not where it goes next.
One · 29 January 2016

January 2016 was the month the circuit breaker arrived and left. The mechanism took effect on the first trading day of the year and was suspended on the eighth, after four triggers in two sessions. The Composite finished the month at 2,737.60, 22.65 percent lower — its worst month in nearly eight years. ChiNext closed at 1,994.07, down 26.53 percent, the largest monthly fall since that index began in 2010. Across the entire market, fifty-two stocks rose in January.

What followed: February was worse in the small print and no better in the total — the Composite fell another 1.8 percent, ChiNext 5.7. March then produced the year’s best month, with the Composite up 11.75 percent and ChiNext up 19.05. The quarter still finished down 15.12 and 17.53. And the low of that whole cycle, 2,638.30, was printed on 27 January — inside the month that set the record, before the rebound, not after it.

Difference worth keeping: January 2016 had a rule that switched the market off twice in four days, and one session in which close to thirteen hundred names finished at their floors. October 2026 has no such rule and no such session. Eighty-six floors against 4,602 declines is a thin tail on a market that traded all day and never stopped. A panic and a repricing both leave marks; they are not the same mark.
Two · 31 October 2022

The last session of October 2022 closed the Composite at 2,893.48, down 0.77 percent, a few points off its own floor. It was the year’s first monthly close below 3,000, and the fourth consecutive month of decline. Turnover that day was ¥396.65 billion — roughly a quarter of what Friday’s session carried.

What followed: November rose 8.91 percent, the largest monthly gain of that year, and the month’s low, 2,896.76, was printed on its first session. The turn arrived with the policy measures of that November — property support, the pandemic rules — which is to say it arrived from outside the tape, not from anything visible in a count of limit-downs.

Difference worth keeping: October 2022 was broad. The whole market was de-rated and the whole market was re-rated. October 2026 is narrow — one industrial chain coming apart while liquor and banks are bid. A broad low and a narrow one are not repaired by the same hands.

Eighty-six names were taken off the board. Four thousand six hundred were merely marked down. The first is a decision; the second is a price. The record keeps them in separate columns.

FOUR
Coda
Last Word

The board at the exchange door is wiped clean every night, and that is the one mercy of this business. Nothing carries over — not the price at which you were wrong, not the sector that ruined the month.

In a kitchen, eighty-six is said out loud so that the whole line hears it and stops sending orders for a thing that is no longer there. Saying it does not put the scallops back. It only stops the waste. October is closed. Along the river the ginkgoes have gone yellow all at once, the way they do in the last week of the month, and nobody on the floor has looked at them.
