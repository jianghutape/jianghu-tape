A daily read of the Chinese tape

Chapter Twenty-Eight · Two Records

by Dan Rivers · 2026-11-16
ONE
Narrative
The Day

Three boards touched the lowest price of their year on Monday. Seven banks and Midea, the white-goods maker, touched the highest price of their lives. The Shanghai Composite, which contains all of them, fell 0.87 percent. That is not a summary of a day. It is where two crowds finished counting at the same moment.

An index can be right about itself and wrong about the room.

The Shenzhen Component’s low of the session was 10,642.37, which is also its lowest reading in fifty-two weeks. ChiNext, the growth board, printed 2,308.90 at its worst, the same number as its fifty-two-week low. The Beijing 50 did it too, at 833.67. Each of the three found the bottom of its year at the bottom of its day, then came back off it. The CSI 300, the large-cap gauge, came within 1.79 points of its own yearly low and stopped there. The selling was not aimed at one name.

Shanghai’s headline number stayed small because the selling was wide at the bottom of the market and shallow at the top of the index. The Composite closed at 3,602.57, down 31.78 points, having traded below 3,600 during the session. ChiNext fell 4.37 percent, the STAR 50 5.16 percent, and the Beijing 50 4.54 percent. Across the whole market 1,136 names rose and 4,344 fell, with 99 unchanged — about one name in five higher. Thirty-three sealed at limit-up, the ten-percent daily cap on most A-shares, and 68 at limit-down, the daily floor; a second published count gives 63 floors, and no exchange figure was available, so the larger number is used here with that note attached. Combined Shanghai-Shenzhen turnover was ¥1.4845 trillion, ¥171.2 billion less than Friday.

The shelf that broke was the one the autumn had made most expensive. Components — printed-circuit boards, copper-clad laminate, connectors — fell 8.707 percent on the Shenwan classification, with Shennan Circuits, Dongshan Precision, Shengyi Technology and Jingwang Electronics all sealed limit-down. Communications equipment fell 8.666 percent; Innolight, the optical-module name whose turnover had led the whole market in each of the previous two sessions, lost more than 12 percent on ¥23.54 billion of trading, again the largest single tally in Shanghai and Shenzhen. Semiconductors lost 6.016 percent and electronic chemicals 5.594 percent. Main-force accounts — the domestic gauge of institutional flow — gave up about ¥27.2 billion across those four shelves alone.

What was bought was older and quieter. Seven banks — Industrial and Commercial, China Construction, Nanjing, Chengdu, Hangzhou, Jiangsu and Qingdao — printed all-time highs, and Agricultural Bank and Bank of Shanghai gained more than 2 percent. Small kitchen appliances rose 2.819 percent: Beiding sealed at the 20-percent ceiling on the growth board and Bear Electric, Joyoung, Changhong Meiling and Vatti at theirs. White goods gained 1.997 percent with Midea’s record. City commercial banks rose 2.201 percent while main-force accounts were net sellers in that bucket — a shelf going up while the measured money left it. Coal mining and insurance also took in small net amounts; the whole market’s main-force gauge printed minus ¥64.38 billion.

There was a mundane fact on the other side of the appliance bid. October exports of household appliances came to 402 million units, up 14.1 percent from a year earlier, worth $8.956 billion, up 20.9 percent; and quarterly growth has risen from 1.5 to 10.5 and 16.0 percent. Those numbers come from the October customs data. A shipment schedule is a dull thing beside a limit-up, and it survives a bad tape.

Hong Kong’s board ran the other way. As of 15:36 in Hong Kong, with the closing auction still to run, the Hang Seng stood at 23,741.08, up 0.44 percent, and the HSCEI up 0.50 percent; Hang Seng Tech was down 0.34 percent. These are intraday readings, not closes. Banks, coal and oil led; semiconductors and hardware sat at the bottom. Friday’s close had been 23,637.60 and 3,813.19 respectively, with HK$2.33 billion of southbound net purchases that day; no Monday figure was available.

TWO
Analysis
Why

Start with the arithmetic of the money, because Monday was the third consecutive session in which the same gauge printed a large negative number. Main-force net flow for the whole market was minus ¥64.38 billion, after minus ¥60.02 billion on Friday and minus ¥52.41 billion on Thursday; together those three days come to about ¥176.8 billion. Earlier in the previous week the same gauge had been positive. The sign changed in the middle of that week and did not change back.

The volume is the more interesting half. Thursday’s decline came on ¥1.932 trillion, the heaviest of the run; Friday’s on ¥1.6557 trillion; Monday took another ¥171.2 billion away. Three sessions of falling prices on shrinking money is not a crowd rushing for the door — it is a crowd that stopped arriving. This letter wrote on Thursday that volume rising on a falling tape is disagreement, not fear: for every seller who accepted a lower price there was a buyer who thought that price was wrong. On Monday there were fewer people willing to hold that argument. A shelf that loses almost nine percent on less money is not being fought over. It is being left.

Disagreement is loud. Absence is quiet, and it takes a while to show up in an index.

The American session that finished before Shanghai opened was Friday 13 November, and it is a coordinate here, not a cause. The S&P 500 lost 0.40 percent and the Nasdaq 0.91 percent while the Dow gained 0.10 percent; the Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 3.43 percent, the weakest of the four, with AMD down 8.75 percent and Broadcom 4.76 percent. October producer prices in the United States rose 6.5 percent from a year earlier, and the ten-year Treasury finished at 5.139 percent. A flat blue-chip index beside a semiconductor index down three and a half percent is the same split Shanghai printed on Monday: the oldest shelves held, the most expensive ones did not. That is a comparison, not a mechanism.

The triggering story was in its fourth day. Nvidia’s board had approved Jensen Huang’s departure on 12 November; on 13 November the shares fell another 2.01 percent. Brokerage notes carried second-hand press reports of a draft American chip bill restricting Chinese companies’ access to American chips in data centres beyond US borders; no official text is cited here. Prices had been answering that question for four sessions.

What held the Composite up was not buying in the expensive names. It was the weight of the old ones. Bank, insurance, coal, appliance and telecom shelves are large in the index and small in the day’s imagination; when they gain two percent and the growth boards lose four or five, the headline number splits the difference. The breadth tells you which side most of the market stood on. An index is an average of weights. A tape is a count of people. On Monday the two disagreed by more than three to one.

Two slower facts sat underneath. Margin financing balances stood at ¥2.2865 trillion as of 13 November, down ¥16.34 billion on the day, and October renminbi lending contracted by ¥100 billion, a figure released after Friday’s close. Neither is a Monday event. They describe how much borrowed money was left to argue with a falling price. A market that prints all-time highs in seven banks while its leveraged balances edge lower is not doing one thing.

Three boards found the bottom of their year at the bottom of their day, and seven banks found the top of their lives. The Composite called it 0.87 percent.

THREE
Historical Parallels
The Record
The record shows where the road has been, not where it goes next.

This session’s snapshot carries no verifiable historical analogue, and I will not manufacture one for a day when three boards printed yearly lows and seven banks printed lifetime highs. What this letter’s own verified archive does hold are two stretches when the same expensive shelf fell hard and the record kept a measurable aftermath. Both are set down here as history only.

One · 27 January to 7 February 2025

On 27 January 2025 Nvidia fell more than 17 percent in New York on the fear that cheaper models would need fewer chips. The mainland exchanges were shut for the Spring Festival; when they reopened on 5 February, the optical line did not wait for a second reading. Innolight closed 14.43 percent lower at 98.25 yuan, barely half the 185.83 it had printed at its October 2024 peak; Eoptolink lost 18.49 percent, and two more names in the same line fell harder still. The AI-application index rose 3.59 percent that day. What followed is the part the record keeps: two sessions later, on 7 February, the leaders recovered between five and 6.7 percent. The fright was shorter than the fall.

Difference worth keeping: 2025 answered an external shock after two weeks of closed doors, with the whole chain down at once and money crossing to an application index. Monday was the fourth consecutive session of the same story, with 68 limit-downs across the market and money crossing to banks, appliances and coal instead.
In 2025 the rebound took two sessions. In 2023 the repricing took six weeks. The archive keeps both clocks.
Two · 16 June to 31 July 2023

The first optical-module run of 2023 offers a longer clock. On 16 June Innolight rose 14.99 percent in a day; four sessions later it closed at 170 yuan, more than five times its price at the start of that year, with a market value of ¥136.5 billion. Then came the bill. On 11 July the company guided first-half profit growth of between 11.69 and 36.05 percent, and the shares fell more than 6 percent the next session; by 31 July the adjusted price stood at 88.56, a quarter below the peak of 118.73. The earnings were good. The price had already spent them.

Difference worth keeping: that run rested on a single customer chain and no domestic order book, and the whole of Innolight’s turnover on 16 June was ¥6.6 billion. On Monday the same name changed hands in ¥23.54 billion, the largest tally in the market, and the selling ran through four hardware shelves at once. Same shelf, a different size of crowd.

The record stands for comparison.

FOUR
Coda
Last Word

At half past four the appliance factories in Shunde are still running, and the seven banks whose shares printed records have locked their doors for the night. A record is a number with a date attached; nothing about it changes the shift pattern. The people assembling a rice cooker on Monday evening did not watch a growth board touch its yearly low, and those who watched it all afternoon could not name a single city bank on the list. Two ends of the same tape, one with a date nobody had seen before and one with a date nobody wanted to see again.

The boards stayed open until three. They always do.
