A daily read of the Chinese tape

Chapter Thirty-Two · The Weight

by Dan Rivers · 2026-11-20
ONE
Narrative
The Day

At 10,256.39 the Shenzhen Component stopped falling because the session stopped. That number was not a low it had touched and walked away from; it was the last print of the day, and it was the lowest close in fifty-two weeks. ChiNext did the same at 2,212.13 and the Beijing 50 at 759.90. Three boards, three yearly floors, none climbed back. On Thursday four indices had touched their lows and come off them on the thinnest money of the year. On Friday five touched them, three stayed there, and more money changed hands.

A fifty-two-week low is a fact about one price. Five of them in one session is a fact about a room.

The Shanghai Composite did not stay. It fell to 3,500.83 in the morning — a fifty-two-week low, and a round number that held — and closed at 3,533.79, down 1.23 percent, thirty-three points above its floor. The CSI 300 broke to 3,894.47 and recovered to 3,954.57, down 0.76. The STAR 50 lost 3.12 percent. But the number that did not fit was the SSE 50: up 0.45 percent, the only one of the seven broad gauges in this fetch to finish higher. Its closing level is not in the verified fetch, so only the change is quoted here. It had been down more than two percent during the session. Banks, insurers and oil weights lifted it in the last hour.

Under those averages, 313 names rose and 5,236 fell. About one price in eighteen finished higher. Thirty sealed at limit-up — the ten-percent daily cap on most A-shares — and 61 at limit-down, the daily floor. A published midday reading had counted six floors; the closing count was sixty-one. Thursday’s had been 649 up and 4,876 down, so Friday was worse on both sides. The Beijing 50 is down 47.25 percent this year, ChiNext 30.94.

Turnover answers the only question a thin day leaves behind — did the money come back? On Friday it did: ¥1.4222 trillion, about ¥117 billion more than Thursday’s ¥1.3051 trillion, which one published count had called the quietest single session of 2026. Shanghai contributed ¥702.6 billion and Shenzhen ¥719.6 billion. This was not another quiet day. It was the day after one, with more money in the room and almost every price going down.

The day after the quietest session of 2026 was not quiet.

The bids were narrow and they were old. State-owned big banks gained 2.52 percent on the published classification used here, all six of them higher, with ICBC up 3.30; insurers gained 1.23 and joint-stock banks 1.18. ICBC, China Construction Bank, Yangtze Power and CITIC Bank printed all-time highs, and PetroChina rose about four percent. There was a dated reason for the first of these: the Finance Ministry had completed a ¥300 billion special treasury bond issue to replenish core tier-one capital at the big banks and four state insurers, with ICBC and Agricultural Bank taking ¥260 billion of it between them. Pork had its own small catalyst — a national hog price of ¥9.36 a kilogram, roughly eight percent above the start of the month — and a hog-chain name sealed at the ceiling.

Everything with a semiconductor in it went the other way. The semiconductor industry lost 4.91 percent, with six of 183 names higher, and drew ¥10.915 billion of main-force net outflow — the domestic gauge of institutional flow — far more than any other named industry. Communications equipment lost 4.71 percent, electronic chemicals 6.37. Other electronics fell 6.99, the weakest of the named industries, and military electronics 6.00. Two optical-module names still did the two largest turnovers in the market, ¥14.941 billion and ¥14.741 billion. One corner refused: components lost 2.94 percent yet took the only large named inflow, ¥1.826 billion, as printed-circuit-board names went to their ceilings against the fall.

Hong Kong’s board fell with it, and later. As of 16:08 in Hong Kong, inside the closing auction window and before a final print, the Hang Seng stood at 22,410.80, down 1.89 percent, Hang Seng Tech at 3,511.14, down 3.78. These are intraday readings, not closes. NetEase fell 17.92 percent after its third-quarter release; Alibaba rose more than three percent late in the day. Southbound flows via Stock Connect reached HK$11 billion by 15:54 — an intraday reading, and a third straight session of net buying.

TWO
Analysis
Why

Start with the shape, because the shape is the argument. An index is a sum with weights attached; breadth is a headcount. On Thursday the two disagreed on the lightest money of the year. On Friday they disagreed again, this time with ¥117 billion more of it. Volume rising on a falling day is not fear, exactly; it is disagreement. For every seller who accepted a lower price there was a buyer who thought that price was wrong, and the tape recorded both. What it does not record is how long either of them intends to stay.

There is a concrete reason the SSE 50 finished higher, and it is not a mystery about sentiment. That gauge is heavy in the largest banks, insurers and oil majors. The ¥300 billion recapitalisation bond was completed this session; a third batch of the swap facility set up to support the capital market had moved from an approved quota into actual purchases concentrated in high-dividend shares and broad index funds. This letter names no fund, describes no product and commends none. A weighted average can be lifted at its largest weights. On Friday the largest weights were the ones with a dated balance-sheet reason to be bought.

A second fact sits under the price action. The central bank drained a net ¥688 billion from the money market on Friday — ¥379.5 billion of overnight reverse repos against ¥1.0675 trillion maturing — after a net injection of ¥67.3 billion the day before. None of this is offered as a cause. It is the setting in which a market with 5,236 decliners found ¥117 billion more turnover than the day before.

The American session that finished before Shanghai opened was Wednesday 19 November — a coordinate, not a cause. All four major gauges fell by roughly two percent. Nvidia lost 3.299 percent to 145.98. The minutes of the Federal Reserve’s October meeting, released in the early hours of 19 November Beijing time, recorded that almost all officials thought a rate increase might be needed in the months ahead. I set these beside Friday’s Shanghai, not in front of it. A price falling in New York overnight does not set one in Shanghai at ten the next morning; it gives the reader a scale.

An index can be lifted by a handful of bids. A market needs five thousand.

Friday’s whole-market main-force gauge printed minus ¥52.08 billion, a seventh consecutive session of net outflow and wider again after Thursday’s minus ¥31.11 billion. Wednesday’s had been the narrowest of the run, about an eighth of Monday’s ¥64.38 billion; Friday’s was more than one and a half times Thursday’s. A narrowing followed by a widening is not a reversal. Within the named industries the money was almost entirely one-sided: semiconductors gave up ¥10.915 billion while components, the sole large inflow, took ¥1.826 billion. The strongest theme on the published concept board gained less than one percent. That is not rotation, in which money moves from one shelf to another. It is a room in which most shelves were left alone.

Two things had not happened when this was written. The American session of 20 November had not begun, and Hong Kong’s closing auction had not produced a final print — which is why every Hong Kong number above carries a clock time and none is called a close. A tape written at the close keeps that discipline. The Fed minutes were a dated document, not a reading of anything that had not happened.

Five boards printed fifty-two-week lows, three closed on them, 5,236 names fell — and the gauge made of the largest banks finished higher.

THREE
Historical Parallels
The Record
The record shows where the road has been, not where it goes next.

Today’s fetch carries no verifiable historical analogue, and I will not manufacture one for a session that printed five yearly lows on rising volume. What this letter’s own verified archive does hold are two stretches where prices fell to a low or a chain broke and the record afterwards kept a measurable outcome. Both are set down here as history only.

One · 5 February 2024

On 5 February 2024 the Shanghai Composite touched 2,635 intraday, with close to 3,000 names at their daily floors at one point and liquidity visibly failing. The next morning Central Huijin said it had widened its purchases of exchange-traded funds, and the regulator suspended new securities lending the same day. The Composite closed up 3.23 percent, the Shenzhen Component 6.22 and ChiNext 6.71, with more than 3,800 names higher on ¥926.3 billion of turnover. What the record keeps is the aftermath: the market rose for two more sessions, and by the last day before the New Year holiday the Composite had climbed roughly 230 points off that low. Friday had official money in it too — a completed recapitalisation bond and a swap facility moving into purchases — but no announcement of widened index-fund buying came with it.

Difference worth keeping: 2024 was a liquidity-failing low with close to 3,000 floors and an official statement attached to the buying; Friday had 61 floors against 30 ceilings, ¥1.4222 trillion of turnover, and no such statement. The 2024 rebound is a fact about 2024. Nothing in that record says anything about Friday’s aftermath, which had not been printed when this was written.
In 2024 the low held and official money arrived with a statement. In 2025 a broken chain rose again two sessions later. The archive keeps both outcomes and starts neither.
Two · 27 January to 7 February 2025

On 27 January 2025 Nvidia fell more than 17 percent in a single session on news of a low-cost Chinese AI model. When the A-share market reopened after the Spring Festival on 5 February, the optical-module chain went to its floors: Innolight fell 14.43 percent to 98.25, about half its 8 October 2024 high of 185.83; Eoptolink lost 18.49, TFC 16.88 and T&S Communications 19.38, while an AI-application index rose 3.59. What the record keeps is the aftermath: by 7 February the three module names had rebounded 5.0, 6.32 and 6.71 percent. Friday’s semiconductor industry lost 4.91 percent and ¥10.915 billion of main-force money, with communications equipment and electronic chemicals beside it, and two module names still topped the turnover list.

Difference worth keeping: 2025 had one named shock and a two-week closure before the selling, and its aftermath was a rebound within two sessions in a market then near its highs. Friday came at the end of a seven-session run of net outflows, with five yearly lows on the board, no single shock and no market closure preceding it. A two-day rebound is a fact about February 2025; the archive has nothing to say about a session it never saw.

The record stands for comparison.

FOUR
Coda
Last Word

At four o’clock a bank branch on a Shanghai corner pulls its shutter down. Two doors along, a pork stall weighs out the last of the day at ¥9.36 a kilogram, roughly eight percent above the start of the month. Neither has a view on an index weight. On Friday five boards printed their yearly lows, three closed on them, 5,236 names fell, and the one gauge made of the largest banks went up.

Somewhere an index fund changed hands in size; the tape kept the volume and not the names. Outside, the stall light was still on. That was all the day promised.
