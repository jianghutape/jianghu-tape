A daily read of the Chinese tape

Chapter Thirty-Seven · The Floor Held

by Dan Rivers · 2026-11-27
ONE
Narrative
The Day

A floor is only a floor until it holds. On Friday the Shanghai Composite fell to 3,467.41 in the morning, twelve points above the lowest price of the previous fifty-two weeks, and then spent the rest of the session going up. It closed at 3,606.49, 3.73 percent higher. The floor from Thursday did not break. Very little else about the day was quiet.

Every major gauge in the fetch finished higher, and the order of the finishes told its own story. Shenzhen Component rose 7.24 percent to 10,814.02. ChiNext gained 11.61 to 2,443.96. Beijing 50 added 13.20 to 851.79. STAR 50 led with 15.07 to 1,346.25, and CSI 300, the heaviest of the set, rose 4.17 to 4,063.06. On Thursday five of these six had printed a fifty-two-week low; on Friday the closest any came to those marks was Shanghai, twelve points clear.

The book underneath was as one-sided as the index sheet. The whole-market count was 5,382 names higher against 181 lower, with 18 unchanged: ninety-six percent of all prices finished up. There were 439 limit-ups — the ten percent daily cap that applies to most A-share stocks — against a single limit-down. On Thursday the same count had been 1,766 higher against 3,642 lower, with 68 limit-ups. Two sessions, one week apart in the calendar and a universe apart in the tape.

Money came back with the prices. The two markets traded ¥1.9998 trillion, ¥929.78 billion in Shanghai and ¥1.070 trillion in Shenzhen, about ¥682.4 billion more than Thursday’s ¥1.3174 trillion. Volume rose on a day when almost every price rose. That is a description, not a verdict: the same arithmetic can accompany different weeks.

The bids were not spread evenly, even on a day this broad. Electronic chemicals led the named industries at 16.30 percent, all thirty-six constituents higher. Semiconductors gained 15.23 with all one hundred and eighty-three higher — a shelf where, the day before, fifty-four of one hundred and eighty-three had managed to close up. Communications equipment added 15.21, all eighty-six higher. Components rose 11.44 with all sixty-one up, software 10.22 with all one hundred and thirty, IT services 11.60 with one hundred and twenty-one of one hundred and twenty-three.

The other side of the list was short and old. Fisheries fell 2.78 percent, with one name higher out of six — the same shelf that had led Thursday at 6.58. Large state banks lost 2.14, none of six higher; joint-stock banks 1.56 with none of nine. Coal mining slipped 1.58 and city banks 1.65. The defensive shelves that had held the weak sessions gave ground on the strong one. I note the swap; I do not grade it.

The path had no drama in it, which was itself the point. Shanghai opened at 3,482.38, near Thursday’s close, made its low at 3,467.41 and finished at 3,606.49, within four points of its high of 3,610.72. ChiNext opened at 2,202.82, closed at 2,443.96 against a high of 2,444.60 — essentially the top of the day. STAR 50 opened at 1,177.48 and ended at 1,346.25 against a high of 1,347.62. A market that had spent a week making lows spent this session closing at highs.

Individual names carried the same arithmetic. A semiconductor equipment maker and an optical-chip name both reached 20.01 percent, the cap for their boards; a coatings stock reached 20.03. These are single prices on a single day. The more useful number sits underneath them: one hundred and eighty-three of one hundred and eighty-three semiconductor names higher.

Analysis
Why

The day’s most quoted item arrived in the news file and was dated Friday. The State Council met on deepening capital-market reform, and the readout named mechanisms rather than prices: improving the stabilisation mechanism, implementing existing policies and planning new ones, pushing listed companies toward larger dividends and buybacks, strengthening the role of the state team and refining Central Huijin’s quasi-stabilisation-fund function with sufficient liquidity. It also spoke of support for long-term money from insurers, enterprise annuities and social-security funds, and of better defences against imported risk. A meeting about the plumbing of the market became the day’s catalyst. I record the date and the words; I do not weigh how much of the move belongs to them.

Whole-market main-force money was a net inflow of ¥107.7 billion, against ¥0.823 billion on Thursday — a swing of about ¥106.9 billion inside one session. That is the largest figure in this run of chapters, and it needs its proportion kept: ¥107.7 billion is roughly five percent of the ¥1.9998 trillion traded. The sign flipped from Thursday, and the size changed with it.

The money followed the shelves that had been weakest. Semiconductors took ¥23.299 billion of main-force inflow, communications equipment ¥15.700 billion, components ¥12.719 billion, securities ¥3.375 billion and general equipment ¥3.239 billion. On the other side, large state banks gave up ¥1.572 billion, joint-stock banks ¥1.518 billion, agricultural processing ¥0.860 billion and white goods ¥0.739 billion. Thursday’s largest outflow was Friday’s largest inflow. That reversal is a fact about two sessions, not a rule.

There is a detail that resists the tidy version. Friday’s readout spoke of dividends, buybacks and long-term money, while the actual bid went hardest into semiconductors and optical links — the same growth shelves that had been sold through the previous week. The policy language and the flow language were not the same language. Where they met was in the decision to take risk at all, which showed up in the limit-up count and in the ¥682.4 billion of extra turnover.

Three dated items sat behind the session. Guangdong published a computing-power plan on Thursday targeting one thousand exaflops by 2028; eight ministries published a services-trade plan the same day; and the communications-network plan from the day before named 400G and 800G links, 1.6T channels and work toward 6G. The four largest state banks have signed share-repurchase lending agreements above ¥600 billion in total, and the margin ratio for financed purchases is set to move from 100 to 80 percent on 30 November. These are entries in the file, dated and unweighted.

The overnight coordinate is the same Wednesday session the previous chapter used, because Thanksgiving closed American cash markets on Thursday. The last American session to finish before Shanghai opened on Friday was 25 November: the S&P 500 at 7,182.78, down 0.2543 percent, the Nasdaq at 23,585.803, down 0.6225, the Dow at 50,182.95, up 0.3284 as the only one of the three higher, and the semiconductor index at 8,774.86, down 0.6526. Nvidia closed at 149.30, down 3.913, and Micron at 601.25, down 4.532. The ten-year Treasury was 4.858 and COMEX gold $3,588.4. American chip prices and Shanghai chip bids moved in opposite directions on Friday; the tape records both and explains neither.

Hong Kong finished its own session before this was written. On 27 November the Hang Seng closed at 22,694.77, up 2.11 percent, Hang Seng Tech at 3,637.88, up 6.45, and the HSCEI at 7,644.18, up 2.01. Southbound money recorded net purchases of about HK$4.587 billion for the full session, against a net outflow of HK$21.351 billion on the previous day, the largest single-day net sale there since April. Hong Kong is another exchange in the same country, trading on a different clock. Its technology shelf and Shanghai’s moved the same way on Friday; that is the whole of the observation.

Two sessions, one week: 3,642 prices down on Thursday, 5,382 up on Friday.

Historical Parallels
The Record
The record shows where the road has been, not where it goes next.

Today’s fetch carries no verifiable historical analogue, and I will not invent one for a session that reversed five days of yearly lows in a single afternoon. Two accounts from this letter’s own verified archive are kept below as records of other days when a policy meeting and a broad reversal appeared together. Neither is a reading of Friday.

One · 5 February 2024

In early February 2024 the Shanghai Composite fell to 2,635 with nearly three thousand stocks at limit-down. Central Huijin then announced an expansion of its ETF holdings. What the record keeps is the aftermath: across 5 and 6 February the index gained 3.23 and 6.22 percent, ChiNext 6.71, on ¥926.3 billion of turnover, and by the close before the holiday the index had recovered roughly 230 points from the low. Friday also had a state-team measure and a reversal after yearly lows. Difference worth keeping: 2024 began with a limit-down washout and ¥926.3 billion of turnover; Friday began with 439 limit-ups and ¥1.9998 trillion. The 2024 path belongs to 2024.

Difference worth keeping: one was a rescue after a washout; the other a reversal without one.
In 2024 a state team met a limit-down wave. In 2022 a midday meeting met a panic. The archive keeps both endings and starts neither.
Two · 16 March to 27 April 2022

On 16 March 2022 a meeting of the financial committee was reported just after midday. The Shanghai Composite, heavy into the afternoon, finished 3.48 percent higher at 3,170.71 with 137 limit-ups on nearly ¥1.2 trillion of turnover. What the record keeps is the aftermath: the reversal did not mark the year’s low. On 27 April the index traded at 2,863.65, well below the March close. Friday also had a committee meeting during the session and a tape that turned. Difference worth keeping: the 2022 move came after a fall of several percent in the same week, and what followed was a lower low five weeks later. I attach no such sequence to Friday.

Difference worth keeping: 2022’s meeting ended a panic; Friday’s followed five quiet days of falling prices.

The record stands for comparison.

Coda
Last Word

The delivery rider who had stopped the week before to look at a fifty-two-week low passed the same screen again on Friday. The number on it had changed so much that he checked the date before he pedalled on.

A floor is a place the price visited. On Friday it stayed visited, and the price went elsewhere. The record keeps both, and chooses neither.
