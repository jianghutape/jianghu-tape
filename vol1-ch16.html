A daily read of the Chinese tape

Chapter Sixteen · Return to Sender

by Dan Rivers · 2026-10-29
ONE
Narrative
The Day

Zero of sixty-one. That is the number Thursday wrote: of the sixty-one listed names in the components sector — the boards and substrates that sit under almost every server rack sold this year — not one closed higher, and several of them finished sealed at their floors. On Wednesday that same sector had risen 4.97 percent, with fifty-nine of those sixty-one names up. The round trip took one night and one morning, and it came back with change.

The semiconductors did it too, in a larger room. One hundred and eighty-three names; one hundred and seventy-seven of them closed higher on Wednesday. On Thursday, seven did. Nothing about the industry's order books was renegotiated in the eighteen hours between those two counts. What changed was the price someone was willing to pay for a story about those order books, and that changed in the dark, on the other side of the Pacific.

Most of the six benchmarks I track printed their high in the opening minute and never saw it again — the Shenzhen Component opened at 11,379.00 and that was its ceiling, and so, to the tick, was the CSI 300’s opening print of 4,075.47. The Shanghai Composite missed its own opening by less than two points. This is what a gap-down-and-slide looks like: no opening rally to sell into, no second chance, just a price that was best at 9:30 and worse at every moment after.

The arithmetic matters less than the shape. The Composite gained 33 points on Wednesday and gave back 30 on Thursday; against Tuesday's close it stands three points higher — two sessions of noise for almost nothing. The ChiNext went further: it added roughly 87 points on Wednesday and handed back 117, finishing more than thirty points below where Tuesday had left it.

Whatever the arithmetic, the shape is the same: the money that arrived on Wednesday left on Thursday, and the tape recorded the exit as carefully as it had recorded the entrance.

Breadth gave the day away early. Fewer than eight hundred names closed higher; more than four thousand seven hundred did not. On Wednesday more than four thousand names had risen; the count inverted in a single session. The limit board flipped with it — thirty-nine ceilings against forty-two floors, on a day when the index moved less than one percent.

What rose instead was the other side of the ledger. The state-owned banks added 2.39 percent with all six of their names higher, and the city commercial banks matched them with all seventeen of theirs. Industrial and Commercial Bank of China and China Construction Bank both set record closes, and at one point during the session the four largest lenders together were worth more than 10 trillion yuan. PetroChina added 3.24 percent to its highest close in eighteen years. Coal mining rose 2.42 and insurance 1.76. Liquor added 1.51, and aerospace — for reasons of its own — 6.84.

Two sessions, one round trip: the Composite is three points above Tuesday's close, having been thirty-three above it in between.
Zero of sixty-one, and several of them sealed at the floor. The reversal took one night.
TWO
Analysis
Why

The overnight session in New York did not do this. The Dow and the Nasdaq both closed slightly higher, and the Philadelphia semiconductor index added 1.75 percent. Whatever reached Shanghai on Thursday morning arrived after those closes, in an earnings release.

Alphabet reported its third quarter after the New York close on 28 October. The headline missed: revenue of $127.6 billion against $128.2 billion expected. Google Cloud did not — it grew 103 percent, to $30.71 billion, and beat. The trouble sat underneath. Earnings per share came in at $2.12 against a consensus nearer $3. Search and related revenue, the engine that funds the build-out, grew 12 percent, to $63.35 billion. The consensus had been nearer $65 billion. Capital expenditure ran at $51.36 billion, up 114 percent year on year. Free cash flow printed at negative $9.07 billion — the second consecutive quarter in deficit. The shares fell close to a tenth in after-hours trading.

The mechanism is worth stating plainly, because it is easy to misread. The market did not decide that artificial intelligence demand had weakened. It decided to re-examine what a dollar of capital expenditure is worth when the spending is financed and the cash flow is negative. The question moved from how much is being built to what the building returns. Microsoft, reporting the same night, made the contrast itself: it beat on revenue, showed Azure still accelerating at 48 percent, and rose about 3 percent after hours. Same industry, same night, opposite verdicts. Chinese optics, board and memory names are priced, in large part, against the capital spending cycle of the large American cloud operators; when that cycle is re-priced on the return side rather than the volume side, the tolerance for high multiples compresses.

The overnight session closed higher. The damage arrived after the close, in a filing.

A second result landed on the same side the next morning. Samsung Electronics became the first technology company ever to book a quarterly operating profit above 100 trillion won — and still missed: net profit of 75.21 trillion won against an 89.06 trillion won consensus. The shares fell more than 5 percent in Seoul. Two large suppliers of the same build-out, two disappointments, one morning.

The interest-rate backdrop did not help. The Federal Reserve held rates at 3.75 to 4.00 percent at its October meeting, as expected, but upgraded its description of the economy from solid to strong, repeated that inflation remains elevated, and recorded two dissents in favour of a 25 basis point increase. The December option was left on the table. For long-duration equity — value that is mostly a promise about years five through ten — a longer stretch of high rates is a mechanical headwind.

Domestic earnings supplied the rest. The third-quarter reporting window is closing, and the tape's tolerance for a near-miss has collapsed. Tianfu Communication reported third-quarter net profit attributable to shareholders of 879 million yuan, up 55.30 percent year on year — a number that would have drawn applause in a different week. It was marked down all the same, as slightly short of a consensus above 900 million. The shares fell more than 19 percent and touched their floor during the session. When positioning is crowded, a quarter that is merely good reads as a quarter that is not good enough.

The flow data shows where the money went instead. Main-force funds left semiconductors to the tune of 13.66 billion yuan, communications equipment 10.85 billion, components 8.91 billion — the three deepest withdrawals of the day, and precisely the three sectors that had taken Wednesday’s largest inflows. They arrived in smaller amounts at aerospace and wind power equipment, and in smaller ones still at the state-owned banks, the insurers and the liquor makers. The defence was not a rotation into a new story. It was money stepping sideways into things that pay.

Turnover fell 72 billion to 1.5565 trillion. That matters for how one reads the damage: a genuine stampede usually prints larger, not smaller. Thursday looked less like panic than like a shortage of bids — buyers did not disappear so much as step back and wait, and the price did the rest of the work on its own.

Hong Kong, on the same clock and under a different exchange, closed lower as well: the Hang Seng finished at 22,958.65, down 0.59 percent. The Hang Seng Tech index closed at 3,744.14 — down 2.84 percent, at a 52-week low.

One scheduling note. Zhongji Innolight and Eoptolink, the two largest optical module names, are due to report on 30 October. Their results were not in the market on Thursday and nothing in this piece is drawn from them.

THREE
Historical Parallels
The Record
The record shows where the road has been, not where it goes next.
One · 21 April 2023

A single policy report — that the White House was preparing an order restricting American investment in Chinese semiconductors, artificial intelligence and quantum computing — was enough to break a crowded trade. The semiconductor sector fell 6.47 percent in one session, to 4,551.30. The Composite dropped 1.95 percent to 3,301.26 — its largest one-day fall of that year. The other benchmarks lost between 1.9 and 4.1 percent. Fewer than six hundred names rose against more than four and a half thousand that fell. Turnover was 1.22 trillion yuan — the fourteenth consecutive session above a trillion.

What followed: the sector did not stabilise on day two. It fell again on each of the next three sessions — a cumulative decline close to 10 percent, per the Jiemian note of 26 April — and the second leg had a different engine entirely: first-quarter earnings, led by JCET, whose net profit fell 87 percent year on year. The break became a slide, and the slide ran on into May.

Difference worth keeping: April 2023 arrived after fourteen straight sessions above a trillion yuan in turnover — a break from a high, a crowded top. Thursday arrived on turnover that shrank 72 billion. And the mechanism differs: 2023 was an outside hand restricting who could fund the build-out, while Thursday was the market re-pricing what the build-out earns. One cuts demand at the source; the other only re-rates the multiple.
Two · 5 February 2025

The first session after the Spring Festival holiday. DeepSeek's open, low-cost model had circulated through the holiday, and the market read it as a threat to hardware volume. The optical and board complex broke: T&S Communications fell 19.38 percent and Eoptolink 18.49. Tianfu Communication lost 16.88 and Victory Giant 14.90; the rest of the chain went down between twelve and fifteen percent, and several printed circuit board leaders sealed at their floors. What rose that day was software and domestic compute: the DeepSeek index itself jumped nearly 14 percent, and application names filled the limit-up board.

What followed: the hardware complex bounced at once — on 7 February the three biggest optical names rose between five and seven percent — and by mid-month the leaders were trading above their 5 February closes. It did not hold. By 31 March the recovery had been handed back, and the April tariff shock then took the leaders to the true lows of that cycle. A break, a bounce, a fade: the tape took six weeks to pick a direction, and the direction it picked was not the bounce.

Difference worth keeping: February 2025 was a structural event, not a market-wide one — the Composite fell only 0.65 percent that day while the broad market rose. The selling was confined to one chain. Thursday was broad: 4,728 names fell and the banking sector set records. Also, the 2025 shock asked whether better algorithms would mean fewer chips bought; Thursday asked whether the chips already being bought are earning their keep. A question about volume and a question about return do not resolve on the same timetable.

Both entries in the file share one mechanism. The tape did not stop believing in the build-out. It stopped accepting the price it had been paying for belief.

FOUR
Coda
Last Word

It is the last Thursday of October, and the plane trees along the bus route have started dropping their leaves in the afternoon rather than the morning, which is how you know the light has shifted. The chestnut sellers at the corner have put out the small iron drums again. None of this has any bearing on a printed circuit board.

But it is worth saying that a tape which rose on Wednesday and fell on Thursday has not contradicted itself. It has simply done the two things it does. The chestnut seller does not ask what yesterday's price was.
