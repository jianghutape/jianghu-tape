A daily read of the Chinese tape

Chapter Thirty-Five · Lower Still

by Dan Rivers · 2026-11-25
ONE
Narrative
The Day

Before eleven in the morning on Wednesday the Shanghai Composite had already spent its high. It opened at 3,522.59, touched 3,526.34, and then walked the other way for the rest of the day, closing at 3,476.47, down 2.03 percent, with its low of 3,468.65 — a fifty-two-week floor — set in the afternoon. ChiNext told the same story through a wider lens: a high of 2,274.18 in the morning, a close of 2,183.58, and no point after the open at which the day’s gain survived. Its close was its low. There was no hidden machinery in any of this. The tape showed the whole walk down.

Six broad gauges were in the fetch, and all six finished lower. Four of them printed a fifty-two-week low intraday: Shanghai at 3,468.65, Shenzhen at 10,086.36, ChiNext at 2,183.58 and the CSI 300 at 3,886.92. The Shenzhen Component closed at 10,086.36, down 3.24 percent, and ChiNext at 2,183.58, down 4.19 — both exactly at their lows for the day. The STAR 50 was the weakest of the set, down 5.12 percent at 1,186.14, though its own low of 1,185.45 stayed above its fifty-two-week mark of 1,132.33. The Beijing 50 fell 4.75 to 755.87. The CSI 300 lost 1.65. Two of the six did not break a yearly floor; four did. That is the claim, and it is the whole claim.

Breadth was uglier than the index arithmetic. The published whole-market count was 586 names higher against 4,926 lower, close to nine in ten prices finishing down. There were 57 limit-ups — the ten percent daily cap on most A-shares — against 44 limit-downs, an unusual detail on a day this red: a small number of names still found their ceiling while the broad market found its floor. A separate Shanghai-Shenzhen-only tally put the session at 567 up, 4,588 down, 58 ceilings and 49 floors; it leaves out Beijing and is not mixed into the count used here. The direction is not in doubt in either series.

Turnover thinned. The two markets traded ¥1.3831 trillion, ¥652.7 billion in Shanghai and ¥730.4 billion in Shenzhen, about ¥272.0 billion less than Tuesday’s ¥1.6551 trillion. That matters as a description, not as an omen. Tuesday’s decline came with more money changing hands than the day before; Wednesday’s came with less. A market can lower its prices on a lighter book, and this one did.

The bids that existed went to small, defensive shelves. Fisheries led the named industries at 4.65 percent, with one stock at its ceiling. Pharmaceutical distribution added 1.09, hotels and catering 1.01, baijiu 0.89 and traditional Chinese medicine 0.88. The large state-owned banks rose 0.60 percent as a group, yet their line recorded a net outflow of ¥0.540 billion — prices up, money out. These are the day’s facts. They are not advice for anyone, and nothing here ranks one shelf above another for anybody’s purposes.

The other side of the list was long. Electronic chemicals fell 8.94 percent, batteries 7.30, semiconductors 7.02, energy metals 5.48, consumer electronics 4.79 and components 4.48. In the semiconductor line only three of 183 names closed higher; in batteries it was one of 97; in electronic chemicals, one of 36. Under the concept labels the same complex appeared as photolithography tools down 7.78, memory chips down 7.30 and advanced packaging down 6.84. The names that had carried the earlier optimism were the names being repriced, in one session, on less money than the one before.

TWO
Analysis
Why

The overnight session belongs here as a coordinate, not a cause. The last American market to finish before Shanghai opened was Tuesday 24 November: the S&P 500 down 0.4985 percent, the Nasdaq 1.478, the Dow up 0.8537 as the only one of the three higher. The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 7.88 percent, giving back part of the previous session’s extraordinary jump; Nvidia lost 5.859 percent, Micron 11.01, TSMC 3.96 and Broadcom 3.977. Gold and copper fell too. I set these beside the Shanghai open for scale. A price fixed in one country overnight does not explain a session in another country seven hours later, and it is not used here to do so.

The budget item is dated and separate. The central bank ran ¥800 billion of one-year medium-term lending and ¥500 billion of seven-day reverse repos against ¥1 trillion of maturing medium-term paper, a net withdrawal of ¥200 billion, alongside ¥617 billion of overnight reverse repos. The exchanges will lower the minimum margin-financing ratio from 100 percent to 80 percent on 30 November. The commerce ministry said it would suspend, from 28 November, controls on certain gallium, germanium and antimony exports to the United States. Elsewhere in Asia, memory shares fell heavily. These sit in the file as facts with dates; none is a lever pulled on Wednesday’s close.

The flow numbers give the rotation a shape. Whole-market main-force money was minus ¥48.96 billion, against minus ¥3.781 billion on Tuesday — a deterioration of roughly ¥45 billion in one session. Semiconductors alone recorded minus ¥12.497 billion, a gap larger than the next four named outflows put together: components minus ¥3.960 billion, batteries minus ¥2.765, communications equipment minus ¥2.547, consumer electronics minus ¥1.709. The largest named inflows were measured in hundreds of millions: baijiu plus ¥0.584 billion, paper plus ¥0.569, general retail plus ¥0.518. The defensive bid was real, but it was a different order of size from the money leaving the hardware complex.

One detail resists the easy reading. Communications equipment fell 2.44 percent and still drew ¥88.49 billion of turnover, the largest of any line in the market, with one optical-module name up 7.65 percent and another trading ¥16.4 billion. Nine of 86 names in that sector closed higher. A sector can lose ground and keep the market’s attention at the same time. Volume stayed where the earlier story had been; the bid did not. That separation, not the index level, is the sharpest fact in Wednesday’s tape.

As of 15:55 in Hong Kong, with the closing auction still to run, the Hang Seng stood at 21,957.38, down 1.18 percent, Hang Seng Tech at 3,373.51, down 2.69, and the HSCEI at 7,380.46, down 0.97. These are intraday readings, not closes. All three had printed fifty-two-week lows during the session — 21,915.88, 3,367.15 and 7,366.65 — after opening near flat and reaching an intraday high more than one percent above the prior close. The previous close, on 24 November, was 22,219.65 and 3,466.87, with southbound net buying of HK$6.203 billion. For 25 November only intraday southbound figures were published, roughly HK$1 billion at 15:31 and HK$3 billion at 15:44; the full-session total was unavailable and none is quoted here.

Four yearly floors and a thinner book: the market reached lower ground with less money underfoot.

Semiconductors lost ¥12.497 billion of main-force money. The largest named inflow, in baijiu, was ¥0.584 billion.

THREE
Historical Parallels
The Record
The record shows where the road has been, not where it goes next.

Today’s fetch carries no verifiable historical analogue, and I will not manufacture one for a session whose defining feature was four yearly lows on less money than the day before. What this letter’s own verified archive does hold are two stretches in which an index reached a low and the record kept a measurable aftermath. Both are set down here as history only.

One · 5 February 2024

On 5 February 2024 the Shanghai Composite touched 2,635 intraday, with close to 3,000 names at their daily floors at one point and liquidity visibly failing. The next morning Central Huijin said it had widened its purchases of exchange-traded funds, and the regulator suspended new securities lending the same day. The Composite closed up 3.23 percent, the Shenzhen Component up 6.22 and ChiNext up 6.71, with more than 3,800 stocks higher on ¥926.3 billion of turnover. What the record keeps is the aftermath: the market rose for two more sessions, and by the last day before the New Year holiday the Composite had climbed roughly 230 points off that 2,635 low. Wednesday had a similar surface — yearly lows, prices at their floors, a thin book — but no official statement arrived with the buying, and 44 floors stood against 57 ceilings rather than close to 3,000. The record shows what happened in 2024; it says nothing about what follows Wednesday.

Difference worth keeping: 2024 was a liquidity-failing low years beneath the market’s highs, with an announced buyer; Wednesday’s turnover was ¥1.3831 trillion and no buyer was announced. The 2024 rebound is a fact about 2024 alone.
In 2024 the buyer was announced. In 2022 the turn was real and the year went lower. The archive keeps both endings and starts neither.
Two · 16 March 2022

On 16 March 2022, after two heavy down days, the market opened weak and turned violently in the afternoon when the State Council’s financial stability committee met on the economy and the capital market. The Composite closed 3.48 percent higher at 3,170.71, ChiNext gained 5.20, more than 4,300 stocks rose and 137 of them reached their ceilings, on turnover of nearly ¥1.2 trillion. What the record keeps is less comfortable: that reversal was never the low of that year. Five weeks later, on 27 April, the Composite fell to 2,863.65 intraday before closing at 2,958.28. A long lower shadow records that somebody bought. It does not record who, or why, or with what staying power. Wednesday had no named meeting, no afternoon reversal and 4,926 declining names against 586 advancing ones.

Difference worth keeping: 2022’s turn had a cause announced during market hours and nearly ¥1.2 trillion of turnover; Wednesday had ¥1.3831 trillion across the whole day and no such cause. A low that later proved not to be the low is a fact about 2022, not a reading of Wednesday’s 3,468.65.

The record stands for comparison.

FOUR
Coda
Last Word

Outside a brokerage on a Shanghai side street the electronic board kept the morning’s high at the top of its scroll long after the price had walked away from it. A man was locking up his bicycle against the rail. Nobody was reading the number by then.

The high was true. So was the close. A record can hold both without choosing between them, which is more than most of us can manage at the end of a day.
