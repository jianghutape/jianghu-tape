A daily read of the Chinese tape

Chapter Twenty-Four · The Remainder

by Dan Rivers · 2026-11-10
ONE
Narrative
The Day

The Shanghai Composite opened at 3,682.04 on Tuesday morning, 0.58 percent above Monday’s close, and the first ten minutes were the best ten minutes of the day. At 3,683.73 the index met something it could not get past and turned around. It then spent five hours and fifty minutes returning the opening gift, a point at a time, and closed at 3,663.72 — a gain of 2.93 points, or 0.08 percent, which is roughly the amount of noise one large order can make.

One large order had already been placed somewhere else. PetroChina, still the heaviest company in Shanghai at 2.163 trillion yuan, had watched Brent settle 5.85 percent lower at $92.57 the night before. It opened at 12.30 against Monday’s 12.62, touched 11.75 in the afternoon and closed at 11.82: down 6.34 percent. PetroChina is 3.37 percent of the Composite’s market value. Multiply the weight by the fall and the answer is 0.21 percent of the index — seven point eight points gone. The index itself produced 2.93.

Ten point seven points, then. 0.29 percent. That is what the Composite did once its heaviest member was lifted out of the sum. The arithmetic is not an argument with the index; it is an explanation of why the front page and the trading desk were reading two different days.

Seven point eight points. One company. That was the whole of Tuesday’s index arithmetic.

Because on the same afternoon, 4,273 stocks rose against 1,149 that fell, with 158 unchanged. Ninety-five names finished at their ceilings — 93 ordinary shares and two under special treatment — and not one finished at its floor. Of the 114 attempts at a limit that day, nineteen broke, a seal rate of 83 percent against Monday’s 56. The tallest board of the session was Minfa Aluminium’s seventh in a row.

What rose, rose along a narrow and almost theatrical line. Film and cinema took 6.80 percent, digital media 3.79, gaming 2.72. Shanghai Film sealed at 42.57 and never traded a cent above it, which is how a strong board closes. Ciwen Media sealed at 9.33 after spending the morning as low as 8.47 — a 10.15 percent climb off its own low. Huace Film & Television went for the 20 percent ceiling in the afternoon, failed to hold it, and finished 16.39 percent higher at an eight-year high.

The news beneath that board was not a rumour. QuestMobile’s autumn report put the net gain in time spent inside short-drama apps in September at 13.329 billion hours — the largest increase of any application category in the country. DataEye’s estimate for the domestic micro-drama market in 2026 is 121 billion yuan, of which AI-made drama is put above 40 billion. A regulator’s guidance the week before — real actors first, AI given rules — was read by the board as a licence rather than a constraint. A concept with an invoice attached is treated differently from a concept without one.

By three o’clock the two ends of the market had stopped speaking to each other. The CSI 300, which owns the big names, added 0.07 percent. The CSI 1000, which owns the small ones, added 1.23. ChiNext, having opened 2.44 percent higher, settled for 1.46.

TWO
Analysis
Why

Start with what did not happen. Nothing was sold in Shanghai on Tuesday. Something was repriced, and it had been repriced in London and New York the night before. The trigger was a sentence: word out of Washington that a resumption of large-scale strikes on Iran was considered extremely unlikely, with reports from the Strait of Hormuz pointing the same way. The war premium stacked into crude over several weeks came out in a single settlement.

Look at the bottom of Tuesday’s board and the names do not look like an industry. Potash fell 2.04 percent, civil explosives 2.02, thermal coal 1.80, coal mining 1.56, shipping 1.34. Potash is fertiliser, explosives are mining, shipping is freight. They are not one business. They are one price — the price of energy, and of everything that only pays when energy is expensive. When that price moves six percent overnight, six unrelated sectors fall the next morning. That is not a sell-off. It is a single position being unwound in public.

The largest outflow of the whole session was ¥706 million. Nobody was running.

The magnitudes give it away. Shipping and ports, the worst of them, gave up 706 million yuan of main-force money; insurance gave up 81 million. A market being abandoned does not leave that quietly. Set the other side beside it — consumer electronics took in 2.31 billion yuan, film and cinema 1.48 — and the day resolves into a transfer rather than a verdict.

Then the volume. Combined turnover was 1.5048 trillion yuan, 22.2 billion more than Monday: an increase of one and a half percent. For 4,273 stocks to close higher on that much more money, the money has to have moved rather than arrived. The split says from where. Shanghai’s turnover shrank by 8.6 billion to 674 billion; Shenzhen’s grew by 30.8 billion to 830.9 billion. The old economy is listed in Shanghai and the growth board is listed in Shenzhen, and on Tuesday one tape got thinner while the other got thicker.

The futures market filed a dissent. The CSI 500 closed 0.86 percent higher at 6,645.89; its front contract settled at 6,576.20, a discount of 69.69 points, against 53 points the day before. The discount widened on a day when the thing it tracks went up. Two prices for the same afternoon, and only one of them believed the breadth.

New York had already staged this argument the night before, in a different language. The Philadelphia semiconductor index rose 6.86 percent to 10,033.93; Micron added 6.25 and Taiwan Semiconductor 3.51; Nvidia fell 4.27 percent to 178.67. The name that had been bought all year was being sold, and the names behind it were being bought. Shanghai did not import that as a chip trade. It imported it as a face trade: consumer electronics up 2.83 percent with more than ten names in the AI-glasses chain sealing their ceilings in the afternoon, optics up 2.52. The part of artificial intelligence a customer can put on and take off was the part this market found easiest to price.

Hong Kong ran the same afternoon on the same clock and arrived at the same two conclusions in a different accent. At 15:36 there, with the closing auction still to run, the Hang Seng stood at 24,534.66, up 0.35 percent — having opened 0.70 percent higher and sagged to 0.29 by the lunch break. Hang Seng Tech was up 0.97 at 4,079.78, sixty-two basis points ahead of the parent index. The buying sat in semiconductors, optical components, circuit boards and contract research; the selling sat in oil, gas, shipping and coal. Same two trades. Same shape: gap, fade, partial recovery.

Underneath all of it runs the slow money. Equity exchange-traded funds have now taken in cash for twenty-five consecutive sessions, since 28 September, more than 620 billion yuan in total — the longest such run on record. Last week alone was 194.769 billion, the second-largest week this market has had. Eighteen active funds each filled their 2 billion yuan caps this month, 36 billion in all. On 9 November the third tranche of the swap facility for brokers, funds and insurers was drawn: eleven of them completed their repo financing and made their first share purchases. None of that explains a single stock on Tuesday. All of it explains why the floor under this market has held for six weeks.

The index spent the day subtracting one company. Everything underneath it spent the day adding.

THREE
Historical Parallels
The Record
The record shows where the road has been, not where it goes next.
One · 28 August 2023

The stamp duty on share transactions was halved on a Sunday night, alongside a package covering the pace of new listings, share sales by controlling holders and margin requirements. Monday opened at 3,219.04, 5.06 percent above Friday’s 3,064.07, with about forty brokers sealed at their ceilings. Within half an hour the brokers were coming off those ceilings — at the worst point nearly eighty percent of them had broken — and the index closed at 3,098.64, up 1.13 percent. It had surrendered four-fifths of the opening. Turnover was 1.1266 trillion yuan, 360.3 billion more than the previous session. More than 3,600 stocks closed higher and about 1,500 lower. Northbound investors sold a net 8.247 billion that day.

What followed: the gap was filled. By 23 October the Composite closed at 2,939.29 — 159 points, or 5.1 percent, beneath the close on the day of the announcement, and 4.1 percent beneath where it had stood before the news. Eight weeks, and no second announcement.

Difference worth keeping: in 2023 the catalyst was domestic, arrived on a Sunday, and nearly doubled the day’s volume — turnover jumped 47 percent in a single session. Tuesday’s catalyst was an oil price set in another market, and turnover rose 1.5 percent. A policy gap and an imported gap are not the same animal, even when both are given back.
In 2023 the gap was handed back inside eight weeks. In 2024 the money was right, eventually.
Two · 2 January to 13 September 2024

Across those eight and a half months stock exchange-traded funds took in more than a trillion yuan net — 1,001.97 billion on Wind’s count to 30 December. In the same stretch the Shanghai Composite fell from 2,974.93 to 2,704.09, a loss of 9.1 percent, with a low of 2,635.09 on 5 February. The exchange’s own review put the year’s ETF net inflow near 1.2 trillion, of which stock funds were more than a trillion: a record, and 110 percent above 2023.

What followed: the year finished at 3,351.76, up 12.67 percent, with a high of 3,674.40 on 8 October. The money was eventually right. It was not right for eight months, and it was not right across two drawdowns of more than ten percent.

Difference worth keeping: in 2024 the buyers were mostly index funds — four CSI 300 funds alone absorbed more than 520 billion — and they bought a market that was falling. Tuesday’s buyers are buying a market that has already climbed from 2,635 to 3,663. The same plumbing, at the other end of the cycle.
FOUR
Coda
Last Word

At three o’clock the exchange prints one number and five thousand others, and the habit of a lifetime is to look at the first one. Tuesday was a small lesson in looking at the second. Ninety-five names at their ceilings, none at their floors, and an index that closed two point nine three points higher: the tape was warm, and the thermometer read room temperature.

The plane trees along the ring road have begun to turn. That much, at least, was never a matter of arithmetic.
