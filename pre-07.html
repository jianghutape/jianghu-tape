ONE What Starts Tomorrow Opening
Tomorrow:
8 Oct 2026.
Chapter One.

Tomorrow this column stops introducing itself and starts keeping a record: one chapter for each trading session, written after the close, dated, and left alone afterwards.

A chapter will carry four things and nothing else. What the overseas markets did while Shanghai was shut. The session on the ground — the closes, the turnover, how many stocks rose and how many fell. One or two passages of history that rhyme with the day. And the date it was written down.

Why after the bell, and not before the open? Because a reading taken after the close can be checked against the day that actually happened, and a reading taken before it cannot be checked against anything. This is a preference for evidence over nerve. It is not a claim that either produces better results.

The last session before the holiday closed at 3,842.19, up 0.31 percent. Chapter One opens from there.

TWO What It Will Not Do Three promises
No forecast.
No advice.
No rewriting.

Three promises, written down once here so that they can be held to later.

It will not predict tomorrow. No levels, no probabilities, no “likely,” no scenarios dressed up as caution. A tape can be described; the next session cannot be.

It will not advise. No recommendation, no instruction to buy or to sell, no target price. The author holds no licence for any of that, and the column has nothing for sale — which is the cleaner reason.

It will not edit itself. If a day's reading turns out to have missed something, the correction goes in beneath it under its own date, and the original stays exactly as it was published. The third promise is the one that costs something: it is easy to promise accuracy and harder to promise not to improve.

None of these are opinions. Each is either a mechanism or a dated record, and each will be reused rather than restated: when tomorrow's chapter mentions a stock sealed at its limit, it will point back to Pre 02 instead of explaining the wall again.

A reader arriving tomorrow can begin at Chapter One. The six pieces above are scaffolding; not one of them has to be read first.

FOUR Coda Why bother
One session,
read once,
written once.

What starts tomorrow is smaller than it sounds: one session, read once, written down once. Some days it will be interesting. Most days it will not be. The value, if there is any, is cumulative — a shelf of dated readings that nobody went back to improve.

One date is worth noting. Chapter One falls two years to the day after the morning Shanghai opened ten percent higher, in the week described in Pre 06. The anniversary is a coincidence. That it is worth noting at all is not.

The record shows where the road has been, not where it goes next.
