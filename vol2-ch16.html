A daily read of the Chinese tape

Chapter Thirty-Six · Five Floors

by Dan Rivers · 2026-11-26
ONE
Narrative
The Day

A price can visit a floor and leave before the bell. On Thursday the Shanghai Composite fell to 3,455.04 in the morning, the lowest print in fifty-two weeks, and then spent the rest of the session walking back. It closed at 3,476.88, up 0.01 percent — a number that looks like nothing until you know where it had been. Five of the six broad gauges in the fetch printed a fifty-two-week low intraday. None kept it. That is the day in one sentence.

Shenzhen broke below ten thousand at 9,979.06 and came back to close at 10,084.04, down 0.02. ChiNext made a low of 2,155.01 before finishing at 2,189.82, up 0.29; the CSI 300 printed 3,877.82 and closed at 3,900.55, up 0.06. Beijing 50 touched 743.25 and ended at 752.44, down 0.45. STAR 50 was the one gauge that did not print a fresh yearly low: its 1,156.57 low sat above its own mark of 1,132.33, and it closed at 1,169.99, down 1.36, the weakest of the six. Three gauges finished higher and three lower.

The path mattered as much as the endpoints. Shanghai opened at 3,490.14 above the previous close, reached 3,497.81, then fell to the 3,455.04 low before repairing the damage into the bell. ChiNext opened at 2,213.12, climbed more than two percent to 2,227.41, dropped to 2,155.01 and still finished higher. Two indices, two versions of one day: an early bid, a break, a late repair.

The book underneath was less balanced than the index arithmetic. The whole-market count was 1,766 names higher against 3,642 lower, so close to two in every three prices finished down. Against that, there were 68 limit-ups — the ten percent daily cap that applies to most A-share stocks — and only 8 limit-downs. A thin majority of prices fell, yet the ceiling count was eight times the floor count. Both facts are true at the same bell.

Turnover thinned again. The two markets traded ¥1.3174 trillion, ¥613.33 billion in Shanghai and ¥704.04 billion in Shenzhen, about ¥65.7 billion less than Wednesday’s ¥1.3831 trillion. The market reached a set of yearly floors on less money than the day before, then climbed off them. Volume fell on a session whose majority of prices also fell; there is no hidden signal in that, only a description of the book.

The bids that did appear gathered in narrow places. Fisheries led the named industries at 6.58 percent with all six constituents higher; precious metals added 4.22 with all eleven higher. Components rose 3.10 on fifty-five of sixty-one names, with two circuit-board stocks at 19.39 and 16.88. Communications equipment gained 2.74, although only forty-seven of eighty-six names took part. Coal mining was up 2.39 with all twenty-six higher.

Elsewhere the list was thin and red at the edges. Airports fell 3.43 with one name higher out of twelve. Sporting goods fell 3.34 with none of twenty higher; biological products fell 2.80 with none of fifty-one. Semiconductors slipped 1.10 with fifty-four of one hundred and eighty-three higher, and lost ¥2.61 billion of main-force money, the largest named outflow of the day. Even there, one advanced-packaging stock closed 18.94 percent higher. A weak shelf is not a uniform shelf.

Analysis
Why

The policy item arrived during the session and named a direction. Three ministries — industry, cyberspace and the development commission — published a plan for the next generation of communications networks, writing in coordinated computing and transmission, 400G and 800G links, 1.6T channels, and work on 6G, optical transmission and satellite communications. By the close, components had taken ¥4.471 billion of main-force money, the largest named inflow in the market, and communications equipment ¥3.723 billion. The two shelves were up 3.10 and 2.74. A plan on paper became a bid in two named lines; it did not become a bid across the market.

Two other dated items sat in the news file. The central bank conducted ¥571.5 billion of overnight reverse repos, fully meeting demand; on Wednesday it had run ¥800 billion of medium-term lending against ¥1 trillion maturing, a net drain of ¥200 billion, alongside ¥500 billion of seven-day repos. Three large banks said on Wednesday evening that they would expand share-repurchase lending, with agreements and commitments above ¥460 billion in total. The margin ratio for financed buying is set to move from 100 to 80 percent on 30 November. These are entries in the file, dated and unweighted; the tape does not turn them into a reading of Thursday.

The aggregate number changed direction. Whole-market main-force money was plus ¥0.823 billion, against minus ¥48.96 billion on Wednesday — a swing of roughly ¥49.8 billion inside one session. That is a smaller absolute number than the headline suggests: ¥0.823 billion is not the return of the money that left, and the day’s majority of prices still fell. But the sign flipped, and the flip landed on the shelves the plan had named. Agricultural processing took ¥1.017 billion and general equipment ¥0.946 billion; precious metals took ¥0.900 billion. On the other side, semiconductors lost ¥2.610 billion and batteries ¥1.726 billion.

One detail resists the tidy version. Semiconductors were the largest named outflow and still held an advanced-packaging name that closed 18.94 percent higher. The same industry can lose money in aggregate while a narrow line inside it attracts bids. On Thursday the wider computation chain split: circuit boards and optical links drew the policy money, while the chip shelf gave some of it back. I record the split; I do not attach a direction to what comes after it.

The overnight session belongs here as a coordinate, not a cause. The last American market to finish before Shanghai opened was Wednesday 25 November: the S&P 500 down 0.2543 percent, the Nasdaq down 0.6225, the Dow up 0.3284 as the only one of the three higher, and the semiconductor index down 0.6526. Nvidia closed 3.913 percent lower after being more than nine percent down intraday; Micron lost 4.532. The ten-year Treasury yield was 4.858 and COMEX gold $3,588.4. American chip prices and Shanghai circuit-board bids moved in opposite directions on Thursday. The tape records both; neither explains the other.

As of 15:44 in Hong Kong, with the closing auction still to run, the Hang Seng stood at 22,307.37, up 1.55 percent, Hang Seng Tech at 3,433.58, up 1.64, and the HSCEI at 7,520.85, up 1.84. These are intraday readings, not closes. The market had opened 1.22 and 2.29 percent higher and reached roughly 2.2 and 3.4 percent by late morning before easing back. Southbound flows were reported as net selling of more than HK$13 billion by 14:48, against net buying of HK$6.434 billion on the previous session; only an intraday figure is available. Hong Kong is another exchange in the same country, on a different clock.

Five yearly lows, five recoveries, and a book in which two of every three prices still finished lower.

Historical Parallels
The Record
The record shows where the road has been, not where it goes next.

Today’s fetch carries no verifiable historical analogue, and I will not invent one for a session defined by five yearly lows that were all erased by the close. Two accounts from this letter’s own verified archive are kept below as records of other days with a policy line and a split tape. Neither is a reading of Thursday.

One · 28 August 2023

On 28 August 2023 the stamp duty on share trading was halved. The Shanghai Composite opened 5.06 percent higher at 3,219.04, gave away four-fifths of that gap, and closed at 3,098.64, only 1.13 percent up. Turnover was ¥1.1266 trillion, ¥360.3 billion above the day before, and more than 3,600 names finished higher against roughly 1,500 lower. What the record keeps is the aftermath: the gap was filled, and by 23 October the index closed at 2,939.29, 5.1 percent below the announcement-day close. Thursday also had a policy document and a close far from the morning extreme, but the shape was inverted — a yearly low reclaimed rather than a large gap surrendered, on ¥190 billion less turnover than Wednesday. Difference worth keeping: 2023 added ¥360.3 billion of new money and closed with more advancers than decliners; Thursday added no such money and closed with 3,642 decliners against 1,766 advancers. The 2023 path belongs to 2023.

Difference worth keeping: 2023’s document was a tax change with an immediate price gap; Thursday’s was an infrastructure plan that lifted two named shelves while the majority of prices fell.
In 2023 a gap was given back. In 2025 a chain was repriced over two sessions. The archive keeps both endings and starts neither.
Two · 27 January to 7 February 2025

On 27 January 2025 Nvidia fell more than 17 percent in one session on news of a low-cost Chinese AI model. When the A-share market reopened after the Spring Festival on 5 February, the optical-module chain went to its floors: Innolight fell 14.43 percent to 98.25, about half its October 2024 high of 185.83; Eoptolink lost 18.49, TFC 16.88 and T&S Communications 19.38. What the record keeps is the aftermath: by 7 February three module names had rebounded 5.0, 6.32 and 6.71 percent. Thursday’s optical and circuit-board names rose on a domestic plan while an American chip benchmark fell overnight — a different arrangement of the same two actors. Difference worth keeping: 2025 had a single named shock and a two-week closure before the selling, and a market then near its highs; Thursday had no closure, five yearly lows printed and reclaimed in one session, and one shelf drawing money while another gave it back. A February rebound is a fact about February 2025.

Difference worth keeping: 2025’s move followed a shock and a holiday gap; Thursday’s followed a published plan and no market closure. No aftermath is claimed here.

The record stands for comparison.

Coda
Last Word

Near the close, the board outside a brokerage on a Shanghai side street still had the morning’s low scrolling past every few seconds. A delivery rider stopped, read the number, and went back to his bicycle. The index above it had already climbed away.
